# Smith Online user guide

Current as of 2026-10-25. Smith Online runs in the browser from a static build. It is a clean-room rebuild inspired by Smith V4.1 by Fritz Dellsperger, extended with ideas from Tonne Software's Elsie and PIEL and from QuickSmith-Web.

Links marked **Try** load a working example, a guided tour or a view into the application when you read this guide in its Help dialog (a table at the end lists all of them). In a plain Markdown viewer they do nothing.

## 1. Screen layout

The same panels are arranged by the width of the window: three columns on a desktop (1100 px and wider), two on a tablet (700 to 1099 px) and the chart with a sheet below it on a phone (below 700 px). The panels hold the same controls everywhere:

- **Network**: the element palette, the Realize and Losses switches, the network from the load (DP1) to the source, and the target card at the end. On a new design the start guide sits at its head (section 2).
- **Chart**: the Smith chart with its controls (Z grid, Y grid, density, labels, Fit) and the schematic strip below it (drag the handle to resize; Settings can move it to a vertical column beside the chart). Each cell of the strip is as wide as its value, shown with three significant digits, so values never run into each other. A datapoint sweep marks every point; beyond 12 points only the first, the last, the point the chain starts from and evenly spread points in between carry an SPn label (a label that would sit on another is skipped, the starting point's label last of all), hovering any marker names it, and the sweep table lists all of them.
- **Numbers**: datapoints table, selected datapoint detail, usable bandwidth, frequency sweep, circles and contours, two-port device and noise parameters.
- **Tools**: Auto match, Optimize band and Tolerance open in the column of the numbers at every width, never over the chart, so the chart keeps its size while a tool is open (section 4).

A panel with nothing to show yet says what is missing and offers the step that fixes it: the empty network offers **Auto match**, the Response view and Optimize band without a usable sweep range offer **Use 0.5 to 2 f**, the S-Plot view without files offers **Open** and **Add the network**, Tolerance without parts lists the three steps of an analysis, and the datapoints table with only the load says that each part adds a row.

Only the chrome moves: menus and popovers fade in, dialogs and sheets come up, segmented controls and switches slide, and Fit brings the chart view home in a fifth of a second. The chart, the tables and the readouts change in the same frame as the value you type or drag. With the system's reduced motion setting on, nothing moves at all; sheets still change height, at once.

**Desktop** (1100 px and wider; wider side columns from 1440 px):

- **Header**, one row at 1440 px, in groups by purpose: on the left the name and the view tabs (Smith chart, Response, S-Plot); in the centre the design strip (design name, design frequency f, source power for the stress columns, reference impedance Z0) with Undo and Redo beside it; on the right three groups, data (File, Examples, Share link), design tools (Auto match, Optimize band) and settings (Tolerance, Settings), then Help and the theme toggle. On a narrower window the groups wrap onto a second row.
- **Left column**: Network, with a running guided tour at its top.
- **Centre**: the chart. A part armed from the palette or datapoint input shows as a banner ("Series L armed: click, or press and drag, to place it" with **Default** and **Cancel**, or "Click the chart to add DP3" with **Done**) in a free corner of the chart's margin, clear of the disc, its rim labels and the chart controls; where there is no free corner (a short window, a zoomed chart) it lies over the status bar under the chart.
- **Right column**: Numbers. An open tool adds the strip **Numbers | Auto match** (or Optimize band, Tolerance) at its top with the tool's close button; switch back to Numbers at any time without closing the tool. A search that runs while Numbers shows keeps its progress bar in the strip, and its header button carries a dot; the header button brings the tool back, and on the tool's own segment it closes it. A search that finishes while Numbers shows says its result in the status bar and leaves a check dot on the tool's segment until it shows again.

**Tablet** (700 to 1099 px): the header as on a desktop, wrapping onto two rows, then the chart on the left (60 percent of the width, at least 400 px) with its schematic strip, and one side column on the right (at least 280 px) with the segments **Network | Numbers**. An open tool is a third segment with its close button in the strip; a running guided tour sits above the segments. Menus open next to their button as on a desktop.

**Phone** (below 700 px):

- **Top bar**: **More** (a sheet with the header's groups: Data with File, Examples and Share link; Settings with Tolerance, Settings and Theme; Help, and Show the start guide once it was closed), the view tabs (Chart, Response, S-Plot), Undo and Redo.
- **Chart**: full width and square under the top bar; the page does not scroll, so the chart stays in view while you edit. Three chips sit on it: the design chip at the top left ("450 MHz · 50 Ω", plus the source power when set; tap it to edit name, f, power and Z0), the grid button at the top right (Z grid, Y grid, density, labels, Fit). The last message shows as a short toast under the top bar. While a finger is on the chart the readout shows in a band across the top of the chart instead of the design chip and the grid button: the pressed point's name, then z and Z on one line and Y and Gamma on the next. An error or refusal stays as a banner over the top of the chart until you dismiss it; on the Response and S-Plot pages the same banner sits under the top bar (a file that could not be read, for example).
- **Schematic strip**: pinned directly under the chart in the Chart view, at every sheet height and on every segment, so the network is always in view. Each part shows the value the network actually uses: the realized value when Realize is on (with a small mark when it was snapped; the ideal value is in its tooltip), else the ideal value; lines and stubs show Z0 and electrical length, transformers their ratio, and a part with a package shows it next to the value ("18 pF 0402"). The strip ends at the Zin terminal with **Zin** and **VSWR**. It scrolls sideways for a long network; tap a part to select it.
- **Sheet**: below the schematic strip, with the segments **Network | Numbers | Tools** and three heights: peek (just the segments), half (up to just below the chart, so the chart and two ladder cards are in view together) and full. Drag the grabber or the sheet's header to move it; a tap on the grabber switches between half and full, and peek is reached by dragging down or, with the grabber focused, the arrow keys; it never closes. At full a strip at the top shows Zin and VSWR; tap it to drop back to half. Network starts with the start guide on a new design, then the palette as **Add part** (two rows of six tiles, open while the network is empty and one row otherwise), the Realize and Losses switches on one row (their options appear when a switch is on) and the network. Numbers holds the numbers panel; the datapoints table shows #, Z, VSWR and RL, and the **Columns** buttons add |Γ|, ∠Γ and Q. Tools lists Auto match, Optimize band and Tolerance with a line on what each does; a tool opens inside the sheet, and its back chevron returns to the list without stopping a running search. While the search runs behind another segment, a thin bar runs along the top edge of the sheet's header; at peek a line with its progress takes the place of the segments (tap it to bring the tool up), from half up the line sits under them. A search that finishes there says its result in a message ("Broadband search done: best worst-case VSWR 1.31") and leaves a check dot on Tools until you look; **Stop** ends a running search and says so. Arming a part from the palette or pressing a marker drops the sheet to peek, and it comes back after the part is placed, the arm is cancelled or the marker is released; the header at peek then shows the banner with **Default** and **Cancel** (or **Done** for datapoint input). A running guided tour takes the header at peek (title, step, Next) and shows its narration from half up.
- **Response and S-Plot** are pages that scroll under the top bar, with the sheet at peek.

- **Status bar** (desktop and tablet): cursor readout (z, Z, Y, Gamma) or the last message, and a quiet link to the source on GitHub at the right end on a desktop (the project is open source under the MIT license). A message about a step you may want to take back carries an **Undo** button for 8 seconds: removing a part or a datapoint, New, opening a design file, loading an example, starting a tour, applying the band optimizer, replacing the network with a Broadband result and applying tuned (or Realize-snapped) values. Undo and Redo name every step they take back ("Undo: New design", "Undo: Moved TP4: Shunt L 2 to 16.8 nH"); a typed value or a switch leaves the status line clear, and Undo still names it ("Undo: Changed f to 450 MHz"). The design name is typed like the other fields: Enter or leaving the field commits it as one step, Escape reverts it. A message that offers something carries an action button, such as **Scale sweep** after a frequency change. On a touch screen, and on a phone, the message shows as a toast instead, for 4 seconds (8 with a button), with the buttons at touch size.
- **Menus and dialogs**: with a mouse, the File, Examples, right-click and suggestion menus open next to their button or the clicked point and flip or shift so they stay inside the window; Up, Down, Home and End move through a menu, a letter jumps to the next entry starting with it, Enter chooses and Escape closes. On a touch screen the same menus open as sheets from the bottom of the screen (drag the grabber up for more room; flick down, tap outside, press the close button or Escape to close), and every dialog fills the screen with its title in a top bar and **Done** to close it. The Dimensions, Winding, Report and Import dialogs can be dragged by their title bar with a mouse, so they can be moved off the part of the chart you are reading; they reopen where you left them until the page is reloaded.
- **Escape**: closes the top menu or dialog first (Help over Settings closes Help, then Settings), then cancels an armed part or datapoint input, then closes the open tool (on a touch screen a previewed solution ends first, with its row, and the next Escape closes the tool), then closes the guided tour. A value field with typed text takes the first Escape to revert it.

**Try**: [Example 5](example:Example5.xmlsc) is a five-element match with a Q contour; switch between the views with [Smith chart](view:chart), [Response](view:response) and [S-Plot](view:splot).

## 2. Starting a design

Press **New** in the File menu or Ctrl+N. New is an undoable step, so no confirmation is asked; the status bar offers Undo for 8 seconds.

The **start guide** at the head of the network, directly above the load card, lists the three steps of a match and ticks each off when it is done: 1 set the load (done once the load differs from Z0 + j0 or a datapoint was added), 2 set the target (done once a target is set: **= Z0** on the target card sets one equal to Z0, and a value typed into its fields counts even when it is 50 at Z0 50; Z0 is the default), 3 match (done once the network has a part). Each step has a button that goes to its control: the load fields, the target card, Auto match. Once all three steps are done it shows them ticked for about two seconds and then closes itself; its close button hides it earlier. Either way it stays hidden in this browser; Help (and More on a phone) has **Show the start guide** to bring it back. It never covers the chart.

- **Load (DP1)**: enter it as R + jX, as G + jB in mS, or as |Gamma| with angle in degrees (selector on the load card), drag DP1 on the chart, or take it from a measured file (Open, then pick a row or send s11 from S-Plot).
- **More user datapoints**: the load card lists DP1..DPn. Add one by typing or with "datapoint input" mode (every click or tap on the chart adds a point at the design frequency). One datapoint is the chain start; the others are drawn hollow. A datapoint sweep applies the network to all of them.
- **Measured load**: **Sweep all** in the import dialog of a Touchstone, CITI, EZNEC or VNA CSV file makes a datapoint sweep over its rows, and the rows become the load across frequency for every band tool. The design frequency picks the nominal point (DP1, the row nearest f); Optimize band, Broadband, Tolerance over the band, the usable bandwidth and the Response view use the whole band. **Back to frequency sweep** keeps the rows but returns the band tools to the fixed load.
  - **The fitted model**: from six rows up the load is a rational model fitted to the reflection coefficient at Z0 (vector fitting with relaxation, Gustavsen and Semlyen; the order is chosen from 2 upward until the residual reaches the noise of the data, at most 30), made passive so it never reflects more than it receives (|Γ| ≤ 1 checked on a dense grid 10 % past the data, with the residues adjusted where it was not). Between the rows the band tools then follow a smooth curve, not straight lines in R and X. A model that misses any row by more than 0.01 in Γ is not used: the load falls back to linear interpolation of R and X, as it does for fewer than six rows.
  - **Its quality** is shown where the rows are used: the import dialog before you sweep ("the 201 rows are fitted, order 6, max residual 0.003 in Γ"), the datapoint sweep section of Numbers ("Load model: fitted, order 4, max residual 0.00061 in Γ (RMS 0.0004)", or "linear interpolation: the fit's residual was 0.04 in Γ (above 0.01)" in the warning colour), and the band tools' load line.
  - **Extrapolation**: the fitted model is followed 5 % past the lowest and highest row and held at its value there beyond; linear interpolation is held at the end rows. A Broadband band that reaches past the data says so in the warning colour. The usable bandwidth stays inside that range and says when an edge reaches the end of the data.
  - **On the chart**: **Show the measured load on the chart** (datapoint sweep section) draws the rows as grey dots and the model across the band as a grey dashed curve, the load before any network.
- **Target**: the dashed card at the source end. Z0 by default (chart centre); type R and X for any other impedance, for example a conjugate match, or press **= Z0** for a target fixed at Z0 (it stays where it is when Z0 changes). The target is drawn as a hollow diamond.

**Try**: [Example 17](example:Example17.xmlsc) applies a four-element network to three datapoints; [Example 5](example:Example5.xmlsc) has a load of 20 - j12 ohm.

## 3. Building the network

- **Palette**: series and shunt L, C, R, line, open stub, short stub, transformer, series RLC, shunt RLC. Click or tap a part, then click or tap on the chart where it should lead, or press, drag to aim (the preview follows) and let go; the dashed reach line shows what the part can reach. A plain click or tap places it at once. Where the part cannot move the point to (a series L only adds reactance, so it cannot go below the point it starts from), the preview is a dashed red mark and letting go there adds nothing; the message says where to aim ("Series L cannot reach that point; aim above the axis") and the part stays armed. While a finger aims on a phone, the band at the top of the chart shows the aimed point and the part's value, and the preview's label stands above the finger. Clicking the palette again, or **Default** in the banner, inserts the default value; **Cancel** or Escape puts it back. Two fingers still pan and zoom while a part is armed.
- **Right-click** a point on the chart (a long press on a touch screen) to add the next part tuned to that point or to remove the part that produced it (the status bar then offers Undo). On empty chart the menu starts with **Suggest a match to here**. Where two points lie within a fingertip of each other, the menu has a row for the other one, so you can switch to it. **Double-click** a point or empty chart (on a touch screen, double tap a marker) for match suggestions towards it: with a mouse, hovering a suggestion previews it and a click adds it; on a touch screen a tap previews and selects it, and an **Add** button on the selected row (or a second tap) adds it. The Auto match lists (Add parts, Broadband) work the same way.
- **Drag** any datapoint: the load moves freely, other points re-solve the part that produced them along its locus. The point nearest the finger is picked (within 22 px on a touch screen, the selected point first when two are equally near), the finger keeps its place on the marker, and a press without movement only selects it, with nothing to undo. While you drag, the readout names the point and shows its own impedance where it lands on its locus ("TP4: z ... Z ..."); the move is one step, named in the message and by Undo ("Moved TP4: Shunt L 2 to 16.8 nH"). A second finger ends the drag where it is, as one step, and starts a pinch.
- **Ladder cards**: value fields accept engineering notation (`3n9`, `2.2p`, `0.25`), sliders tune 0.2x to 5x with a Reset button (a drag, or key presses less than half a second apart, is one step, named "Tuned Series L 2 to 12.0 nH"), Delete removes. To reorder, drag a card by its header row (on a touch screen, long press it first) and let go where it should go; the other cards move aside to show the slot, and near the top or bottom edge of the panel the list scrolls. A move is one step, named in the message and by Undo ("Moved Series C 2 to position 4"). The arrows (Move up and Move down in the card's menu on a touch screen) do the same one step at a time. **Drag a card out to remove it**: while the pointer is outside the panel that holds the list (the left column, the side column on a tablet, the sheet on a phone) the card turns red with a Remove mark and the list closes the gap; let go there (on the chart, on another panel, even outside the window) and the part is removed, with a message naming it and **Undo**; bring it back into the panel before letting go, or press Escape, and nothing changes. Lines and stubs have Z0, electrical length, permittivity and loss per metre; the Dimensions button turns them into track width and physical length on a substrate or coax. Inductors have a winding helper (air-core solenoid or toroid with AL value) that writes a note on the card.
- **Realize**: snaps every value to an E series (E6 to E96) or to your own stock list (H, F and Ω say which parts a value is for; the IEC 60062 resistor codes go to the resistors only: the R notation 4R7, 10R and R47 (0.47 Ω), the k and M notation 4k7 and 1M5, and a value with a k or M and no unit, 1k or 10k, since no inductor or capacitor is 1000 H or 1000 F; a value without a unit counts for all three only when no value in the list has a unit, a resistor code not counting as one; units other than H, F and Ω, such as 100 MHz, are skipped. Values are separated by semicolons, tabs, line breaks or commas. A comma between digits is read as a decimal comma, 8,2 nH, only in a list that uses semicolons, tabs or line breaks, has units and uses no comma as a separator; elsewhere it may be a separator without a blank, so the value is skipped and named: 4,7n in a list of commas alone, 1,5 alone, 10,22 in a two-column paste, 47,100 pF under a line 10 nH, 22 nH. A comma before exactly three digits, 1,000 pF, may be a thousands separator and is skipped in every list), with scope (all, L, C, R, L-C pairs with preserved resonance) and a lock per part. The realized network is drawn dashed; the datapoints table toggles between ideal and realized; sliders step through realizable values.
- **Losses**: default Q for inductors and capacitors with a per-part override, evaluated like any other element.
- **Package**: each L and C card has a Pkg selector (0201 to 1206, wire-wound coil for inductors) and shows the part's self-resonant frequency once a package is chosen; see section 8.

**Chart gestures**, with a mouse and on a touch screen:

| Gesture | Mouse | Touch |
|---|---|---|
| Pan | drag on empty chart | one-finger drag on empty chart |
| Zoom | wheel, about the pointer | pinch, about the midpoint of the fingers; two fingers moved together pan |
| Fit | Fit button | Fit in the grid sheet on a phone; the Fit button above the chart on a tablet |
| Readout | move the pointer over the chart | a finger down shows it in the band at the top of the chart on a phone, in the status bar on a tablet |
| Drag a point | drag the marker | drag the marker; the nearest within 22 px is picked and named as the finger lands |
| Place an armed part | click, or press, aim and let go | tap, or press, aim and let go |
| Suggestions | double-click a point or empty chart | double tap a marker; on empty chart, Suggest a match to here in the long press menu |
| Context menu | right-click | long press (400 ms), on a marker or on empty chart; the marker pulses |
| Select a point | click | tap |

The zoom sets the half side of the view, which goes from 0.05 to 4 disc radii; at Fit it is about 1.18, so the chart zooms about 24 times in and 3.4 times out. Fit moves the view home over 200 ms (at once with reduced motion); a pan or zoom during that move takes over from it. A long press is cancelled by moving more than 10 px or by a second finger; in the margin outside the disc it does nothing and the finger pans. On a touch screen the markers are 12 px across (the hollow user datapoints too, picked within the same 22 px) and their labels slightly larger.

## 4. Matching

**Auto match** (header, or Tools on a phone) opens in the column of the numbers, behind the strip Numbers | Auto match, so the chart keeps its size and stays in view (section 1). A tool opened during a guided tour ends the tour; the message offers Undo to bring back the design you had before the tour. It offers three methods towards the target and two distributed designers:

- **Add parts**: closed-form L networks, or Pi and T at a chosen loaded Q, ranked by Q; hover previews, click appends. Each network is listed once: an L-network whose second part comes out at zero is the single part, listed one time. With Realize on, the list shows snapped values and the VSWR after snapping. The message names what was added, the same as for a match suggestion, and Undo repeats it.
- **Tune existing parts**: keeps the network and solves the values of the ticked parts so Zin lands on the target; reports exact or the nearest reachable point.
- **Broadband**: searches ladder topologies up to a maximum element count over the sweep band, ranks them by worst VSWR over the band and element count, and shows the Bode-Fano floor when the load is a measured set of datapoints (a single R + jX load has no floor). Over a measured load the band starts as the measured band, and the panel names the load and its model ("Load: the 101 measured points of antenna.s1p, 600 MHz to 2.4 GHz, fitted, order 4, max residual 0.00061 in Γ"). The floor says how it was found: for a fitted load whose model is one reactance with a resistance across the band (series RL or RC, parallel RC or RL) it is Fano's exact bound with the element values read from the model ("exact for a series RL load, from the fitted model"); for any other fitted load it is the Bode-Fano bound with the load Q taken from the model's exact slope at the band centre ("from the fitted model: load Q 1.21 at band centre from its exact slope (Yaghjian-Best), an estimate"). Over linear interpolation the floor is read from the band points as before, and not quoted below 9 of them. Runs in a worker with progress counted in the same ladders as the note above it (a large search then refines its best ladders, counted on their own); hover previews, click applies. The search keeps running while you switch to Numbers; **Stop** ends it ("Stopped the broadband search"). A band typed with From above To is swapped. When the design changes after a run, the list goes and a note says so with its **Run again** button (the only one then); a new run puts the focus on the panel's first field.
- **Transformer**: multi-section quarter-wave transformers from the resistance of Zin to the target resistance, binomial (maximally flat) or Chebyshev (equal ripple at the chosen maximum |Γ|), by the small-reflection theory. The list offers 1 to 6 sections, each with the fractional bandwidth over which |Γ| of the built lines stays under the limit; every section is a quarter wave at f0. The theory is first order, so for a step above about 2:1 the textbook Chebyshev design ripples above the limit; the tool then synthesizes the sections for a lower ripple so the built lines stay under the limit across the band, and says so. A reactance in Zin is not removed; cancel it first. An even-order Chebyshev transformer ripples at f0 instead of landing exactly on the target.
- **Stub filter**: a Butterworth or Chebyshev (ripple in dB) filter of order 1 to 9 at a cutoff frequency, in the design's Z0. The low-pass is exact: Richards' transform of the lumped prototype and the Kuroda identities give shunt open stubs separated by lines. Every stub and line is an eighth wave at the cutoff (a quarter wave at twice the cutoff, where the response has its infinite-attenuation pole), so the response repeats from three times the cutoff on. The high-pass uses shunt shorted stubs joined by Z0 lines of the same length; its cutoff is exact but its passband shape is the classic approximation, and it passes up to three times the cutoff. The table lists every impedance from the load and marks those outside 15 to 150 Ω as hard to build. An even-order Chebyshev low-pass ends in a load other than Z0, which the tool states. Clicking adds the filter in one undo step and, when the sweep is off, switches it on over a range around the cutoff so the Response view shows the curve.
- Both tools give the line and stub lengths in wavelengths at the design frequency, like every other line, so a cutoff or f0 away from f shows as a shorter or longer length. Changing the design frequency afterwards keeps the electrical lengths, as for any line, so the cutoff or f0 moves with it. Both tools keep their settings for the session.

**Optimize band** improves the values of the existing parts for the lowest worst-case VSWR over the sweep range, or for the limit areas when any are set. Over a measured load it works over the measured band against the load model, and names both with the floor from the fitted model. Without a usable sweep range it offers **Use 0.5 to 2 f**, an octave on each side of the design frequency.

**Usable bandwidth** (Numbers) reports the span around f over which VSWR stays below a limit; the Response view shades it. Over a measured load the load is read per frequency from its model and the search stays inside the data (and the 5 % past it): an open edge then means the band reaches the end of the measured data, and a design frequency outside the data has no usable band.

**Try**: the tours [L-network match](tour:l-network), [Single stub match](tour:single-stub), [Pi and T networks at a chosen Q](tour:pi-t), [Broadband match with an element cap](tour:broadband) and [Bandwidth and the band optimizer](tour:band-optimizer); [Example 7](example:Example7.xmlsc) is a Pi match.

## 5. Response view

Plots versus frequency: gain and return loss (S21, S11), VSWR, input impedance, phase and group delay, with up to 8 markers, a cursor readout and a linear or log axis. The plots use the sweep range whether or not the sweep is on; without a usable range (a start or stop of zero, or the two equal) the view offers **Use 0.5 to 2 f**. A start typed above the stop, or a stop below the start, is swapped when it is committed, with a message ("Swapped the sweep to 250 MHz to 1 GHz"); the Response view, Optimize band and Broadband read a range the same way. On a touch screen drag sideways on a plot to move the cursor, with its values in a row under the plot; a vertical swipe scrolls the page and a tap sets a marker. The S-Plot graphs work the same way. **Limit areas** are rectangles a curve must stay out of; breaches are flagged and the band optimizer can use them as its cost. **Overlays** store named snapshots of the curves (up to 10) for comparison.

Over a measured load (a datapoint sweep of a file) the plots span the measured band: the curves are the network on the load model, at least 401 points with every measured frequency among them, and the dots are the network on each measured row itself, so the residual of the model shows where a dot leaves its curve. A note over the plots names the model. **Load alone** in the toolbar adds |S11| and VSWR of the load without the network, dashed, to show what the network improves.

On a phone a sideways drag on a plot moves the cursor, a tap sets a marker, and the readout row under each plot shows the values; the readout table with the cursor and marker columns follows the plots in the page, so it never covers a plot.

**Try**: [Example 16](example:Example16.xmlsc?view=response) opens a network with a 10 to 450 MHz sweep in the Response view.

## 6. S-Plot view

An s-parameter viewer for Touchstone, CITI, EZNEC and VNA CSV files (add them with Add file, with Open in the File menu, or by dropping them on the view; several files at once give one message, such as "Added 2 files; broken.s2p could not be read: ...", and a `.txt` or `.csv` table above 20 MB is refused before it is read): file list with colours, graphs of s11, s12, s21, s22 in dB and phase, return loss, K and mu, MAG or MSG, a polar plot of s21 and s12, s11 and s22 on a Smith chart, and a gain table per frequency that highlights the selected row in every graph. Export converts to Z, Y, H or ABCD parameters (normalized or not) as Touchstone files or CITI to Touchstone. "To the Smith chart" sends s11 or s22 as the load or as a datapoint sweep, or uses the file as the two-port device for the circles.

**Add the network** (in the file list, or Add network to S-Plot in the File menu) plots the design's own network as a two-port referenced to Z0, as built (realized values, losses and packages when those are on), over the sweep range when a frequency sweep is on, else 0.5 f to 2 f. The entry is marked live and follows your tuning a moment after each change; Add the network again replaces it, and **Freeze** keeps it as a snapshot so you can compare before and after. The S-Plot list is not part of the design, so Undo does not touch it; removing an entry asks nothing, and its message carries an Undo for 8 seconds that puts the entry back.

**Try**: [open the S-Plot view](view:splot) and drop a Touchstone file on it, or use Open in the File menu.

## 7. Circles and contours

VSWR circles and constant-Q contours take a number each. With a two-port device (typed or from a file, read at the design frequency) the panel draws input and output stability circles, available, operating and unilateral gain circles, and noise figure circles from NFmin, Gamma opt and rn.

**Try**: [Example 13 input](example:Example13-input.xmlsc) draws stability, gain and noise circles for an amplifier; the tour [LNA input match with circles](tour:lna-input) builds one step by step.

## 8. Analysis for building

- **Component packages**: choose a package per inductor or capacitor on its card (0201, 0402, 0603, 0805, 1206; wire-wound coil for inductors). The chart then draws a third, dotted locus with diamond markers for the network as built: a chip capacitor gets its series inductance, a chip inductor its parallel capacitance and series resistance, and every part the capacitance of its pads to ground. The legend under the chart toolbar names the three loci (ideal, realized, with packages); with Realize on, the packages are applied to the realized values. The card shows the part's self-resonant frequency, the Response view plots the as-built curves dotted, the report adds a datapoint table naming the packages, and the BOM gets a Package column. The parasitics table with its typical values and a source note per row is in Settings; edit it to match the parts you buy. Pick "none" on a card to leave a part ideal.
- **Stress**: with a source power set, each card shows peak voltage and RMS current; limits per part kind flag exceedances, and the BOM carries the same columns.
- **Tolerance (Monte Carlo)**: random part values within their tolerances (default from the active E series), N seeded runs in a worker; the cloud of input impedances on the chart, a VSWR histogram and the yield against a VSWR limit. Judged over the band with a measured load, each run is measured over the measured band against the load model (the cloud stays at the design frequency, on the nominal row). When the design changes after a run, the cloud fades with "stale" written over it and a note with **Run again** sits under the result (the only Run again then). On the phone at half, the histogram scrolls into view when a run ends.

**Try**: the tour [Losses and component packages](tour:packages) turns the package locus on; [Example 15](example:Example15.xmlsc) has a lossy line.

## 9. Files, export and sharing

- **Open**: `.xmlsc` (Smith V4.1 projects, all 22 original examples are also in the Examples menu), Smith Online `.json`, Touchstone `.s1p` to `.s4p`, CITI `.citi` or `.cti`, EZNEC `Lastz.txt`, VNA CSV exports (`.csv`, `.txt`, see below).
- **VNA CSV and tables** (`.csv` and `.txt`, in Open and in the S-Plot view's Add file):
  - Keysight/Agilent ENA and PNA exports: `!` header lines, then `BEGIN CH1_DATA` ... `END` blocks with a column header such as `Freq(Hz),S11(REAL),S11(IMAG)`; several traces per block and several blocks of the same sweep are merged (a row dropped in one block is dropped in all), and a block the reader cannot use, or one at other frequencies, is skipped with a note.
  - Any comma, semicolon, tab or space separated table with a header row: a frequency column (`Freq`, `Frequency`, `f`, with the unit in the header such as `Freq [MHz]` or on the numbers such as `1.5 GHz`; Hz when neither says) and S11 to S22 as re/im, mag/angle or dB/angle pairs (`S11 dB`, `S11 Phase (deg)`, `S21_real`, `S(2,1)`, `dB(S(2,1))` as HFSS and ADS write it; angles in degrees unless the header says rad), or `R` and `X` (also `Impedance Real`, `Zin Re`) columns for an impedance in ohm. A header written as a `#` comment above the rows, as numpy writes it, is used. Y, Z, H and G parameter columns, admittance, transmission, return loss, SWR and delay columns are skipped with a note; the import dialog lists the notes, the S-Plot file tooltip carries them, and a refusal names them.
  - NanoVNA-Saver and LibreVNA style tables: frequency, re, im triples per trace, value columns named only Re and Im, Mag and Phase or Gamma Real and Gamma Imag (a column such as `Phase` joins the parameter before it, else it is taken as S11, then S21; the same parameter given as re/im and as magnitude and angle is read once, from re/im, with a note), and headerless `f re im` rows.
  - Lines starting with `!` or `#` are comments. With semicolons, tabs or spaces a comma inside a number is a decimal comma; in a comma separated file a quoted cell such as `"0,5"` may hold one, `"1,000,000"` is read with thousands separators, and an ambiguous `"1,400"` is not read (the row is dropped). The reference impedance is read from a line such as `Reference impedance: 75 Ohm`, `Z0 = 75` or `# Hz S RI R 75`, else 50 Ω is assumed and the description says so. Rows are sorted by frequency. Rows at zero, negative or non-finite frequency, rows repeating a frequency and rows with a value that is not a number (or one too large to convert) are dropped, and the description says how many and why. A file without a frequency column, without S-parameter or R and X columns, or with fewer than two usable rows is refused with a message that names what is missing.
  - Touchstone text saved as `.csv` or `.txt` (an option line such as `# MHz S MA R 50` or a bare `#` above plain rows, or a Touchstone 2 `[Version]` keyword) is read as Touchstone, with its unit and format; a faulty option line such as `# GHz S MA R 50 Ohm` is reported by the Touchstone reader. A `#` line that is a column header makes the file a table.
  - A file with S11 only goes to the import dialog: the row nearest the design frequency is highlighted, a click makes a row the load, Sweep all makes a datapoint sweep. A file with S21, S12 or S22 as well also goes to the S-Plot list (Show in S-Plot in the dialog); the parameters it lacks show as gaps, the file card and the gain table name them, and Use as device and Save Touchstone need all four. A file with S11, S21, S12 and S22 fills the two-port panel as a Touchstone file does. A file without S11 goes to the S-Plot list only, and the status line says so.
  - A `.txt` file is tried as a VNA table first and as an EZNEC `Lastz.txt` second (an EZNEC table, recognized by its `Src #` column, goes to the EZNEC reader); when neither reads it the message gives both reasons.
- **Save**: Smith Online `.json` (everything), Smith V4.1 `.xmlsc` (L, C, lines and stubs, user datapoints; opens in the original).
- **Export**: chart as SVG or PNG, datapoints as CSV, Touchstone CKT netlist, ASCII datapoints and circles in the original's layout, bill of materials (Realize on; a Package column when parts name one), SPICE `.cir`, LTspice `.asc`, the network as `.s2p` (as built, with realized values, losses and packages, over the sweep when a frequency sweep is on, else 0.5 f to 2 f; SPICE and LTspice use the same range but leave the package parasitics out, and when a part names a package both say so in a comment at the top: "Component package parasitics are not included; see the .s2p export"), Add network to S-Plot (the same network as a live S-Plot entry, see section 6; not undoable, as the S-Plot list is not part of the design), and a Report (chart, schematic, datapoints, circles as one SVG or a print page). Print prints the last report selection; when the browser blocks pop-ups it falls back to the native print dialog of the page and says so. Copy chart as image and copy datapoints as text go to the clipboard.
- **Share link** puts the whole design in the URL and copies the link; on the desktop the button shows a check and **Copied** for a moment once it is on the clipboard, keeping its width; on the phone (Share link in the More sheet) the toast confirms it. Where the browser refuses the clipboard, the status line says the link is in the address bar.

## 10. Settings, keyboard and examples

- **Help**: the question mark button in the header (Help in the phone's More sheet, or F1, or ? when no text field has the focus) opens this guide in a dialog with a table of contents; the arrow and page keys scroll it, Escape or a click outside closes it. Once the start guide was closed, the dialog has **Show the start guide**. The small ? next to a panel heading or the Auto match title opens the guide at that section. Hovering a panel heading or a chart toolbar button shows a one-line tip. Links marked **Try** in the guide, and the index at its end, load a working example, a guided tour or a view.
- **Settings**: colours for grids, datapoints, sweep, circles and the element palette, line widths, label density, default frequency and Z0 for new designs, schematic direction, and the component package table (ESL, Cp, ESR and pad capacitance per package, with a reset per row). Stored in the browser.
- **Theme**: follows the light or dark setting of the operating system by default; the theme button (Theme in the phone's More sheet) cycles auto, dark and light and is named for the theme it is on ("Theme: follows the system"), and the choice is kept in the browser. The switch is instant on the chart; the panels cross-fade.
- **Keyboard**: F1 or ? help, Ctrl+N new, Ctrl+O open, Ctrl+S save as Smith Online `.json`, Ctrl+E opens the File menu at its Export section (Cmd in place of Ctrl on a Mac), Alt+1, Alt+2 and Alt+3 switch to the Smith chart, Response and S-Plot views (Option on a Mac; the File menu lists them; Ctrl+Alt, which is AltGr on Windows, is left to the keyboard layout), Ctrl+Z undo, Ctrl+Y or Shift+Ctrl+Z redo (the status line repeats what the step did, for example "Undo: Added DP2: 30.00 + j10.00 Ω at 2 GHz"), Delete removes the selected part, Escape closes the top menu or dialog, then cancels an armed part, then closes the open tool, then the tour (section 1). In a menu, Up, Down, Home, End and the first letter of an entry move, Enter chooses. Tab stays inside an open dialog.

## 11. Known limitations

- The package parasitics are typical values per package size, not the data of a particular part; the table in Settings is the place to enter datasheet figures. The SPICE, LTspice, netlist and `.xmlsc` exports carry the values and losses but not the parasitics, and Monte Carlo, stress and the matching tools work on the network without them.
- The LTspice `.asc` writer has not been opened in LTspice; the SPICE netlist and the `.s2p` writer are covered by parse-back tests.
- The fitted model of a measured load is passive only where it was checked: on a grid of 20 points per row (at least 2001) from 10 % below to 10 % above the data, each local maximum refined; a resonance narrower than the grid could still peak above |Γ| = 1 between its points. Where the residues cannot make the model passive, the whole model is scaled down, which shows as a larger residual (and then as linear interpolation when it passes 0.01).
- The Bode-Fano floor of a fitted load is exact only for a load whose model is one reactance with a resistance across the band; otherwise it is an estimate from the load Q at the band centre (the model's exact slope, not a finite difference of noisy rows), which can be off either way for a load of low Q or with several resonances in the band. The real frequency technique of depth phase D7 is the answer for those.
- The usable bandwidth search keeps its documented fallback (a search range that excludes the centre frequency is widened, not refused) and may miss a spike narrower than its coarse step.
- Changing the design frequency without a frequency sweep on is accepted without a word; the Response view keeps its own range.
- When a project whose start datapoint is not among the sweep loads is saved as `.xmlsc`, the start is added to the loads of the file.
- A typed value or a switch does not describe itself in the status line (it clears it); Undo and Redo name it.
- Pressing Escape in the Monte Carlo panel clears the cloud; run it again to redraw.
