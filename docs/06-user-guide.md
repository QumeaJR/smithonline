# Smith Online user guide

Current as of 2026-10-26. Smith Online runs in the browser from a static build. It is a clean-room rebuild inspired by Smith V4.1 by Fritz Dellsperger, extended with ideas from Tonne Software's Elsie and PIEL and from QuickSmith-Web.

Links marked **Try** load a working example, a guided tour or a view into the application when you read this guide in its Help dialog (a table at the end lists all of them). In a plain Markdown viewer they do nothing.

## 1. Screen layout

The same panels are arranged by the width of the window: three columns on a desktop (1100 px and wider), two on a tablet (700 to 1099 px) and the chart with a sheet below it on a phone (below 700 px). The panels hold the same controls everywhere:

- **Network**: the element palette, the Realize, Losses and Board switches, the network from the load (DP1) to the source, and the target card at the end. On a new design the start guide sits at its head (section 2).
- **Chart**: the Smith chart with its controls (Z grid, Y grid, density, labels, Fit) and the schematic strip below it (drag the handle to resize; Settings can move it to a vertical column beside the chart). Each cell of the strip is as wide as its value, shown with three significant digits, so values never run into each other. A datapoint sweep marks every point; beyond 12 points only the first, the last, the point the chain starts from and evenly spread points in between carry an SPn label (a label that would sit on another is skipped, the starting point's label last of all), hovering any marker names it, and the sweep table lists all of them.
- **Numbers**: datapoints table, selected datapoint detail, usable bandwidth, frequency sweep, circles and contours, two-port device and noise parameters.
- **Tools**: Auto match, Optimize band, Tolerance and Build open in the column of the numbers at every width, never over the chart, so the chart keeps its size while a tool is open (section 4; Build in section 8).

A panel with nothing to show yet says what is missing and offers the step that fixes it: the empty network offers **Auto match**, the Response view and Optimize band without a usable sweep range offer **Sweep 0.5 f to 2 f**, the S-Plot view without files offers **Open** and **Add this design's network**, Tolerance without parts lists the three steps of an analysis, and the datapoints table with only the load says that each part adds a row.

Only the chrome moves: menus and popovers fade in, dialogs and sheets come up, segmented controls and switches slide, and Fit brings the chart view home in a fifth of a second. The chart, the tables and the readouts change in the same frame as the value you type or drag. With the system's reduced motion setting on, nothing moves at all; sheets still change height, at once.

**Desktop** (1100 px and wider; wider side columns from 1440 px):

- **Header**, one row at 1440 px, in groups by purpose: on the left the name and the view tabs (Smith chart, Response, S-Plot); in the centre the design strip (design name, design frequency f, source power for the stress columns, reference impedance Z0) with Undo and Redo beside it; on the right the groups data (File, Share link), design tools (Auto match, Optimize band, Tolerance, Build) and settings (Settings), then help (Learn, Help and the theme toggle). On a narrower window the groups wrap onto a second row.
- **Left column**: Network, with a running guided tour at its top.
- **Centre**: the chart. A part armed from the palette or datapoint input shows as a banner ("Series L armed: click, or press and drag, to place it" with **Default** and **Cancel**, or "Click the chart to add DP3" with **Done**) in a free corner of the chart's margin, clear of the disc, its rim labels and the chart controls; where there is no free corner (a short window, a zoomed chart) it lies over the status bar under the chart.
- **Right column**: Numbers. An open tool adds the strip **Numbers | Auto match** (or Optimize band, Tolerance, Build) at its top with the tool's close button; switch back to Numbers at any time without closing the tool. A search that runs while Numbers shows keeps its progress bar in the strip, and its header button carries a dot; the header button brings the tool back, and on the tool's own segment it closes it. A search that finishes while Numbers shows says its result in the status bar and leaves a check dot on the tool's segment until it shows again.

**Tablet** (700 to 1099 px): the header as on a desktop, wrapping onto two rows, then the chart on the left (60 percent of the width, at least 400 px) with its schematic strip, and one side column on the right (at least 280 px) with the segments **Network | Numbers**. An open tool is a third segment with its close button in the strip; a running guided tour sits above the segments. Menus open next to their button as on a desktop.

**Phone** (below 700 px):

- **Top bar**: **More** (a sheet with the header's groups: Data with File, Part library and Share link; Settings with Settings and Theme (Tolerance is among the tools of the Tools segment); Help with Learn, Help, Show the start guide once it was closed, and Licences), the view tabs (Chart, Response, S-Plot), Undo and Redo.
- **Chart**: full width and square under the top bar; the page does not scroll, so the chart stays in view while you edit. Three chips sit on it: the design chip at the top left ("450 MHz · 50 Ω", plus the source power when set; tap it to edit name, f, power and Z0), the grid button at the top right (Z grid, Y grid, density, labels, Fit). The last message shows as a short toast under the top bar. While a finger is on the chart the readout shows in a band across the top of the chart instead of the design chip and the grid button: the pressed point's name, then z and Z on one line and Y and Gamma on the next. An error or refusal stays as a banner over the top of the chart until you dismiss it; on the Response and S-Plot pages the same banner sits under the top bar (a file that could not be read, for example).
- **Schematic strip**: pinned directly under the chart in the Chart view, at every sheet height and on every segment, so the network is always in view. Each part shows the value the network actually uses: the realized value when Realize is on (with a small mark when it was snapped; the ideal value is in its tooltip), else the ideal value; lines and stubs show Z0 and electrical length, transformers their ratio, and a part with a package shows it next to the value ("18 pF 0402"). The strip ends at the Zin terminal with **Zin** and **VSWR**. It scrolls sideways for a long network; tap a part to select it.
- **Sheet**: below the schematic strip, with the segments **Network | Numbers | Tools** and three heights: peek (just the segments), half (up to just below the chart, so the chart and two ladder cards are in view together) and full. Drag the grabber or the sheet's header to move it; a tap on the grabber switches between half and full, and peek is reached by dragging down or, with the grabber focused, the arrow keys; it never closes. At full a strip at the top shows Zin and VSWR; tap it to drop back to half. Network starts with the start guide on a new design, then the palette as **Add part** (two rows of six tiles, open while the network is empty and one row otherwise), the Realize and Losses switches on one row (their options appear when a switch is on) and the network. Numbers holds the numbers panel; the datapoints table shows #, Z, VSWR and RL, and the **Columns** buttons add |Γ|, ∠Γ and Q. Tools lists Auto match, Optimize band, Tolerance and Build with a line on what each does; a tool opens inside the sheet, and its back chevron returns to the list without stopping a running search. While the search runs behind another segment, a thin bar runs along the top edge of the sheet's header; at peek a line with its progress takes the place of the segments (tap it to bring the tool up), from half up the line sits under them. A search that finishes there says its result in a message ("Broadband search done: best worst-case VSWR 1.31") and leaves a check dot on Tools until you look; **Stop** ends a running search and says so. Arming a part from the palette or pressing a marker drops the sheet to peek, and it comes back after the part is placed, the arm is cancelled or the marker is released; the header at peek then shows the banner with **Default** and **Cancel** (or **Done** for datapoint input). A running guided tour takes the header at peek (title, step, Next) and shows its narration from half up. When a sheet (File, More) or a full-screen dialog (the part library) covers the page, the tour's head stays on top of it with its buttons, and a control the step points at inside the sheet is scrolled into view.
- **Response and S-Plot** are pages that scroll under the top bar, with the sheet at peek.

- **Status bar** (desktop and tablet): cursor readout (z, Z, Y, Gamma) or the last message, and quiet links to the source on GitHub and to the **Licences** (the MIT license of Smith Online and the notices of the third-party code it includes) at the right end on a desktop; on a phone **Licences** is in the More sheet under Help. A message about a step you may want to take back carries an **Undo** button for 8 seconds: removing a part or a datapoint, New, opening a design file, loading an example, starting a tour, applying the band optimizer, replacing the network with a Broadband result and applying tuned (or Realize-snapped) values. Undo and Redo name every step they take back ("Undo: New design", "Undo: Moved TP4: Shunt L 2 to 16.8 nH"); a typed value or a switch leaves the status line clear, and Undo still names it ("Undo: Changed f to 450 MHz"). The design name is typed like the other fields: Enter or leaving the field commits it as one step, Escape reverts it. A message that offers something carries an action button, such as **Scale sweep** after a frequency change. On a touch screen, and on a phone, the message shows as a toast instead, for 4 seconds (8 with a button), with the buttons at touch size.
- **Menus and dialogs**: with a mouse, the File, Learn, right-click and suggestion menus open next to their button or the clicked point and flip or shift so they stay inside the window; Up, Down, Home and End move through a menu, a letter jumps to the next entry starting with it, Enter chooses and Escape closes. On a touch screen the same menus open as sheets from the bottom of the screen (drag the grabber up for more room; flick down, tap outside, press the close button or Escape to close), and every dialog fills the screen with its title in a top bar and **Done** to close it. The Dimensions, Winding, Report and Import dialogs can be dragged by their title bar with a mouse, so they can be moved off the part of the chart you are reading; they reopen where you left them until the page is reloaded.
- **Escape**: closes the top menu or dialog first (Help over Settings closes Help, then Settings), then cancels an armed part or datapoint input, then closes the open tool (on a touch screen a previewed solution ends first, with its row, and the next Escape closes the tool), then closes the guided tour. A value field with typed text takes the first Escape to revert it.

**Try**: [Example 5](example:Example5.xmlsc) is a five-element match with a Q contour; switch between the views with [Smith chart](view:chart), [Response](view:response) and [S-Plot](view:splot).

## 2. Starting a design

Press **New** in the File menu or Ctrl+N. New is an undoable step, so no confirmation is asked; the status bar offers Undo for 8 seconds.

The **start guide** at the head of the network, directly above the load card, lists the three steps of a match and ticks each off when it is done: 1 set the load (done once the load differs from Z0 + j0 or a datapoint was added), 2 set the target (done once a target is set: **= Z0** on the target card sets one equal to Z0, and a value typed into its fields counts even when it is 50 at Z0 50; Z0 is the default), 3 match (done once the network has a part). Each step has a button that goes to its control: the load fields, the target card, Auto match. Once the load is set and the network matches (Zin at the design frequency within the VSWR limit of the target, or of Z0 while no target is typed, so the common 50 Ω path needs no target), it folds to one row, "Matched. Next:", with the next steps (check tolerance, or optimize over the band with a measured load, make it buildable, more workflows); a part that leaves Zin off the target keeps the steps open. Its close button hides it; it then stays hidden in this browser; Help (and More on a phone) has **Show the start guide** to bring it back. It never covers the chart.

- **Load (DP1)**: enter it as R + jX, as G + jB in mS, or as |Gamma| with angle in degrees (selector on the load card), drag DP1 on the chart, or take it from a measured file (Open, then pick a row or send s11 from S-Plot).
- **More user datapoints**: the load card lists DP1..DPn. Add one by typing or with "datapoint input" mode (every click or tap on the chart adds a point at the design frequency). One datapoint is the chain start; the others are drawn hollow. A datapoint sweep applies the network to all of them.
- **Measured load**: **Use all N rows as the load** in the import dialog of a Touchstone, CITI, EZNEC or VNA CSV file makes a datapoint sweep over its rows, and the rows become the load across frequency for every band tool. The design frequency picks the nominal point (DP1, the row nearest f), and DP1 follows f: changing the design frequency moves it to the row nearest the new f, so the chart, the datapoints table, the Response view and the usable bandwidth agree (the status line names the row; Using the rows says when it moved the design frequency to the nominal row). Optimize band, Broadband, Tolerance over the band, the usable bandwidth and the Response view use the whole band. **Use the fixed load** keeps the rows but returns the band tools to the fixed load. With a measured load the load card shows a line naming the file, its rows, band and model, with **Match over the band** (Auto match, Broadband) and **Show on chart**. **Try**: [the measured antenna workflow](workflow:measured-antenna).
  - **The fitted model**: from six rows up the load is a rational model fitted to the reflection coefficient at Z0 (vector fitting with relaxation, Gustavsen and Semlyen). The order grows a pole pair at a time while no model follows the rows within 0.01 in Γ, and once one does, until the residual reaches the noise of the data or stops improving over three pole pairs; at most 30, and a third of the rows below 20 rows (one real pole fewer is taken when it does as well). The model is made passive so it never reflects more than it receives (see the limits below). Between the rows the band tools then follow a smooth curve, not straight lines in R and X. A model that misses any row by more than 0.01 in Γ is not used: the load falls back to linear interpolation of R and X, as it does for fewer than six rows.
  - **Fits the rows, not the load**: few or noisy rows let a fit put a sharp resonance between two rows that no row sees. A resonance narrower than the spacing of the rows around it is dropped from the model, and the chosen model is checked between the rows against their own straight-line interpolation (and, below 30 rows, by leaving three rows out and fitting again): where it strays by more than 0.01 plus the change of the data over that interval, a lower order is taken, and when none passes the load falls back to linear interpolation ("the best fit strays 0.36 in Γ from the rows near 1.09 GHz, where they are too far apart to show it").
  - **Fitting in the background**: the fit runs off the main thread (a few thousand rows can take half a second); until it is ready the load is the linear interpolation of the rows and the panels say "fitting the model", then every view that uses the load updates.
  - **Its quality** is shown where the rows are used: the import dialog before you sweep ("the 201 rows are fitted, order 6, max residual 0.003 in Γ"), the datapoint sweep section of Numbers ("Load model: fitted, order 4, max residual 0.00061 in Γ (RMS 0.0004)", or "joined by linear interpolation of R and X; the best fit missed a row by 0.11 in Γ (limit 0.01)" and "joined by linear interpolation of R and X; 3 points, a fit needs 6" in the warning colour), and the band tools' load line.
  - **Extrapolation**: the fitted model is followed 5 % past the lowest and highest row and held at its value there beyond; linear interpolation is held at the end rows. A Broadband band that reaches past the data says so in the warning colour, and a band wholly outside the data is refused (the load there is only a held constant). The usable bandwidth stays inside the measured data (the 5 % past it is an extrapolation and is not reported as band) and says where an edge reaches the end of the data.
  - **On the chart**: **Show the measured load on the chart** (datapoint sweep section) draws the rows as grey dots and the model across the band as a grey dashed curve, the load before any network, with a "Measured load" entry in the chart legend. Like the grid options it stays as set for the session, whichever design is open.
- **Target**: the dashed card at the source end. Z0 by default (chart centre); type R and X for any other impedance, for example a conjugate match, or press **= Z0** for a target fixed at Z0 (it stays where it is when Z0 changes). The target is drawn as a hollow diamond.

**Try**: [Example 17](example:Example17.xmlsc) applies a four-element network to three datapoints; [Example 5](example:Example5.xmlsc) has a load of 20 - j12 ohm.

## 3. Building the network

- **Palette**: series and shunt L, C, R, line, microstrip, open stub, short stub, transformer, series RLC, shunt RLC. Click or tap a part, then click or tap on the chart where it should lead, or press, drag to aim (the preview follows) and let go; the dashed reach line shows what the part can reach. A plain click or tap places it at once. Where the part cannot move the point to (a series L only adds reactance, so it cannot go below the point it starts from), the preview is a dashed red mark and letting go there adds nothing; the message says where to aim ("Series L cannot reach that point; aim above the axis") and the part stays armed. While a finger aims on a phone, the band at the top of the chart shows the aimed point and the part's value, and the preview's label stands above the finger. Clicking the palette again, or **Default** in the banner, inserts the default value; **Cancel** or Escape puts it back. Two fingers still pan and zoom while a part is armed.
- **Right-click** a point on the chart (a long press on a touch screen) to add the next part tuned to that point or to remove the part that produced it (the status bar then offers Undo). On empty chart the menu starts with **Suggest a match to here**. Where two points lie within a fingertip of each other, the menu has a row for the other one, so you can switch to it. **Double-click** a point or empty chart (on a touch screen, double tap a marker) for match suggestions towards it: with a mouse, hovering a suggestion previews it and a click adds it; on a touch screen a tap previews and selects it, and an **Add** button on the selected row (or a second tap) adds it. The Auto match lists (Add parts, Broadband) work the same way.
- **Drag** any datapoint: the load moves freely, other points re-solve the part that produced them along its locus. The point nearest the finger is picked (within 22 px on a touch screen, the selected point first when two are equally near), the finger keeps its place on the marker, and a press without movement only selects it, with nothing to undo. While you drag, the readout names the point and shows its own impedance where it lands on its locus ("TP4: z ... Z ..."); the move is one step, named in the message and by Undo ("Moved TP4: Shunt L 2 to 16.8 nH"). A second finger ends the drag where it is, as one step, and starts a pinch.
- **Ladder cards**: value fields accept engineering notation (`3n9`, `2.2p`, `0.25`), sliders tune 0.2x to 5x with a Reset button (a drag, or key presses less than half a second apart, is one step, named "Tuned Series L 2 to 12.0 nH"), Delete removes. To reorder, drag a card by its header row (on a touch screen, long press it first) and let go where it should go; the other cards move aside to show the slot, and near the top or bottom edge of the panel the list scrolls. A move is one step, named in the message and by Undo ("Moved Series C 2 to position 4"). The arrows (Move up and Move down in the card's menu on a touch screen) do the same one step at a time. **Drag a card out to remove it**: while the pointer is outside the panel that holds the list (the left column, the side column on a tablet, the sheet on a phone) the card turns red with a Remove mark and the list closes the gap; let go there (on the chart, on another panel, even outside the window) and the part is removed, with a message naming it and **Undo**; bring it back into the panel before letting go, or press Escape, and nothing changes. Lines and stubs have Z0, electrical length, permittivity and loss per metre; the Dimensions button turns them into track width and physical length on the board's stackup or on coax (see **Microstrip** below). Inductors have a winding helper (air-core solenoid or toroid with AL value) that writes a note on the card.
- **Realize**: snaps every value to an E series (E6 to E96) or to your own stock list (H, F and Ω say which parts a value is for; the IEC 60062 resistor codes go to the resistors only: the R notation 4R7, 10R and R47 (0.47 Ω), the k and M notation 4k7 and 1M5, and a value with a k or M and no unit, 1k or 10k, since no inductor or capacitor is 1000 H or 1000 F; a value without a unit counts for all three only when no value in the list has a unit, a resistor code not counting as one; units other than H, F and Ω, such as 100 MHz, are skipped. Values are separated by semicolons, tabs, line breaks or commas. A comma between digits is read as a decimal comma, 8,2 nH, only in a list that uses semicolons, tabs or line breaks, has units and uses no comma as a separator; elsewhere it may be a separator without a blank, so the value is skipped and named: 4,7n in a list of commas alone, 1,5 alone, 10,22 in a two-column paste, 47,100 pF under a line 10 nH, 22 nH. A comma before exactly three digits, 1,000 pF, may be a thousands separator and is skipped in every list), with scope (all, L, C, R, L-C pairs with preserved resonance) and a lock per part. A third source is **Part library**: each L and C becomes a real part from your part library (section 8), the one nearest by effective reactance at the design frequency. The realized network is drawn dashed; the datapoints table toggles between ideal and realized; sliders step through realizable values. The switch row under the network reads **Realize**, **Losses** and **Board**, the modes of the live loop. While one is on, a line under the switches starts with its name and says what it uses, with an Edit link that opens Build at its section: the series stays inline for Realize, then the scope ("Realize E24 All parts · Edit realization…"); Losses shows the default Q ("Losses Q 50 (L), 500 (C) · Edit losses…"); Board the laminate, vias and traces ("Board RO4350B, 0.762 mm, 1 via per shunt part, traces 1 mm · Edit board…"). The scope, the stock list, the default Q, the stress limits and the board are edited in Build (section 8). **Try**: [Make it buildable](workflow:buildable).
- **Losses**: default Q for inductors and capacitors with a per-part override, evaluated like any other element.
- **Microstrip**: a track of a width and a physical length on the stackup of the design's board (Build, Board), evaluated at every frequency with the microstrip model: Hammerstad-Jensen for the static Z0 and effective permittivity with the copper thickness, Kirschning-Jansen for the dispersion of the effective permittivity and Jansen-Kirschning for that of Z0, conductor loss with the skin effect and the copper roughness (Hammerstad-Jensen roughness factor, up to twice the smooth loss), and dielectric loss from the stackup's loss tangent, which the Djordjevic-Sarkar model carries to every frequency. Unlike a line, its electrical length, Z0 and loss change with frequency the way a real track's do. A new microstrip has the width for Z0 at the design frequency and is an eighth of a wavelength long. A microstrip is 0 to 1 km long wherever its length comes from (the field, the tune slider, a drag, Dimensions, Optimize band, centring) and in every link, file and autosave, so a new one at a very low frequency starts at 1 km and the slider and a drag stop there; the card has **l** and **w** (the schematic strip shows them in the same order, "l 20 mm w 3.12 mm"), shows Z0, εeff and the loss at the design frequency ("Z0 50.47 Ω, εeff 3.1688, 0.030 dB on FR-4, 1.6 mm"; the tooltip gives the static values and says when the width or frequency is outside the range of the dispersion formulas, 0.1 ≤ W/h ≤ 10, εr ≤ 18 and h/λ0 ≤ 0.1, the range the Qucs technical documentation gives for the Jansen-Kirschning Z0 dispersion; the Kirschning-Jansen εeff dispersion is stated for 0.1 ≤ W/h ≤ 100, εr ≤ 20 and h/λ0 ≤ 0.13), and its slider tunes the length. The width takes 1 µm to 1 m with W/h from 0.001 to 1000. The element keeps a copy of the stackup it was made on, so a file or a share link evaluates the same in any browser; when the design's board is on another stackup the card offers **Use the design's stackup**, which keeps what the line does (the width solved again for its Z0 at the design frequency and the length rescaled for the same electrical length; the message names the Z0 and the new width and length), and **Keep size**, which keeps the width and length and says the Z0 that results (a 50 Ω line on 1.6 mm FR-4 moved to 0.508 mm RO4003C keeps its 3.1 mm width and drops to about 24 Ω). In the Dimensions dialog of a line, **Use microstrip** replaces the line by a microstrip of the width and length shown. A microstrip cannot be saved in `.xmlsc` (Smith V4.1 has no such element); the Touchstone CKT export writes it as a lossy line with its Z0, delay and loss at the design frequency, and the SPICE export as an ideal line with its Z0 and delay there, the loss and the dispersion dropped (the netlist says so).
- **Package**: each L and C card has a Package selector (0201 to 1206, wire-wound coil for inductors) and shows the part's self-resonant frequency once a package is chosen; see section 8.

**Chart gestures**, with a mouse and on a touch screen:

| Gesture | Mouse | Touch |

A long press is cancelled by moving more than 10 px or by a second finger; in the margin outside the disc it does nothing and the finger pans. On a touch screen the markers are 12 px across (the hollow user datapoints too, picked within the same 22 px) and their labels slightly larger.

## 4. Matching

**Auto match** (header, or Tools on a phone) opens in the column of the numbers, behind the strip Numbers | Auto match, so the chart keeps its size and stays in view (section 1). A tool opened during a guided tour ends the tour; the message offers Undo to bring back the design you had before the tour. It offers three methods towards the target and two distributed designers:

- **Add parts**: closed-form L networks, or Pi and T at a chosen loaded Q; hover previews, click appends. Each network is listed once: an L-network whose second part comes out at zero is the single part, listed one time. With Realize on, the list shows snapped values and the VSWR after snapping. The message names what was added, the same as for a match suggestion, and Undo repeats it. **Rank by** orders the list by **Q** (lowest loaded Q, the widest bandwidth, first; the default with Realize off), **Yield** or **VSWR** (the default with Realize on), and a line under the notes says how it is ranked. Whatever the ranking, each row has a second line with the network's estimated yield and the part that dominates its spread ("yield about 73 %, Shunt L 1 dominates (59 %)"). The yield is 200 seeded Monte Carlo runs of the whole network (the parts already in the ladder and the new ones), built and judged exactly as the Tolerance panel builds and judges it after Add: the loss model when Losses is on, the realized values when Realize is on, the measured load over the band, its VSWR limit, towards the target, at the design frequency or over the band when Tolerance judges over the band (thinned to 21 points), with its distribution, reel correlation, seed and default tolerance (section 8); locked parts stay exact. On the same seed it is the panel's own pass count over its first 200 runs. Two hundred runs give the yield to a few percent, so it is shown in whole percent ("about 77 %"), as "≥ 99 %" when every run passed and "≤ 1 %" when none did; the tooltip gives the count. The line under the notes says where the limit comes from ("within VSWR 1.5, set in Tolerance"); the Tolerance panel shows its limit and tolerance fields also with no parts in the ladder, so both can be set before the first Add. By yield, ties go to the less sensitive network (the smaller linearized spread of Γ), then the lower Q, and when every row ties the line says the order then comes from the spread; by VSWR, the nominal VSWR judged the same way, lowest first, and when every row ties (each network matched at f) the line says the order comes from Q. The Tolerance panel gives the yield with its interval.
- **Tune existing parts**: keeps the network and solves the values of the ticked parts so Zin lands on the target; reports exact or the nearest reachable point.
- **Broadband**: searches ladder topologies up to a maximum element count over the sweep band, ranks them by worst VSWR over the band and element count, and shows the Bode-Fano limit when the load is a measured set of datapoints (a single R + jX load has no floor). Broadband uses the load every band tool uses: the rows of a datapoint sweep, the fixed load after Use the fixed load (the panel says the rows are kept). Over a measured load the band starts as the measured band, follows a new import while you have not typed a band of your own, and the panel names the load and its model ("Load: the 101 measured points of antenna.s1p, 600 MHz to 2.4 GHz, fitted, order 4, max residual 0.00061 in Γ"). The floor says how it was found: for a fitted load whose model is one reactance with a resistance across the band (series RL or RC, parallel RC or RL) it is Fano's exact bound with the element values read from the model ("exact for a series RL load, from the fitted model"); for any other fitted load it is the Bode-Fano bound with the load Q taken from the model's exact slope at the band centre ("from the fitted model: load Q 1.21 at band centre from its exact slope (Yaghjian-Best), an estimate"). Over linear interpolation the floor is read from the band points as before, and not quoted below 9 of them. A part the search left at the end of its range where it does next to nothing (a series part at the smallest reactance, a shunt part at the largest) is dropped and the network scored again, and each network shape is listed once with the parts it really has. The worst VSWR of every candidate is measured on a dense grid across the band (the band points and at least 201 evenly spaced points; the count is in the result line), and Optimize band prints its worst VSWR on the same grid. Runs in a worker with progress counted in the same ladders as the note above it (a large search then refines its best ladders, counted on their own); hover previews, click applies. The search keeps running while you switch to Numbers; **Stop** ends it ("Stopped the broadband search"). A band typed with From above To is swapped. When the design changes after a run, the list goes and a note says so with its **Run again** button (the only one then); a new run puts the focus on the panel's first field. **Try**: [Broadband limits and multi-section](workflow:broadband-limits).
- **Transformer**: multi-section quarter-wave transformers from the resistance of Zin to the target resistance, binomial (maximally flat) or Chebyshev (equal ripple at the chosen maximum |Γ|), by the small-reflection theory. The list offers 1 to 6 sections, each with the fractional bandwidth over which |Γ| of the built lines stays under the limit; every section is a quarter wave at f0. The theory is first order, so for a step above about 2:1 the textbook Chebyshev design ripples above the limit; the tool then synthesizes the sections for a lower ripple so the built lines stay under the limit across the band, and says so. A reactance in Zin is not removed; cancel it first. An even-order Chebyshev transformer ripples at f0 instead of landing exactly on the target.
- **Stub filter**: a Butterworth or Chebyshev (ripple in dB) filter of order 1 to 9 at a cutoff frequency, in the design's Z0. The low-pass is exact: Richards' transform of the lumped prototype and the Kuroda identities give shunt open stubs separated by lines. Every stub and line is an eighth wave at the cutoff (a quarter wave at twice the cutoff, where the response has its infinite-attenuation pole), so the response repeats from three times the cutoff on. The high-pass uses shunt shorted stubs joined by Z0 lines of the same length; its cutoff is exact but its passband shape is the classic approximation, and it passes up to three times the cutoff. The table lists every impedance from the load and marks those outside 15 to 150 Ω as hard to build. An even-order Chebyshev low-pass ends in a load other than Z0, which the tool states. Clicking adds the filter in one undo step and, when the sweep is off, switches it on over a range around the cutoff so the Response view shows the curve.
- Both tools give the line and stub lengths in wavelengths at the design frequency, like every other line, so a cutoff or f0 away from f shows as a shorter or longer length. Changing the design frequency afterwards keeps the electrical lengths, as for any line, so the cutoff or f0 moves with it. Both tools keep their settings for the session.

**Optimize band** improves the values of the existing parts for the lowest worst-case VSWR over the sweep range, or for the limit areas when any are set. The worst VSWR it prints, now and after a run, is measured on the dense grid Broadband scores on, so both tools give one number for the same network (the sweep section's line is the worst over its own points: the rows of a datapoint sweep, or the sweep points, and says so). Over a measured load it works over the measured band against the load model, and names both with the floor from the fitted model. Without a usable sweep range it offers **Sweep 0.5 f to 2 f**, an octave on each side of the design frequency.

**Usable bandwidth** (Numbers) reports the span around f over which VSWR stays below the design's VSWR limit, judged towards the target when one is set (Z0 otherwise), as the start guide, the next steps and Tolerance judge; with a target the readout says "VSWR towards the target". The Response view shades the band of its VSWR curve, which is referred to Z0. The limit is one number per design, saved with it: the same field as Tolerance's VSWR limit, used by Auto match's yield, the next steps and the lessons too, so changing it in one place changes it everywhere (its tooltip says so). A new design starts with the limit of Settings, New designs (2 unless changed). A limit is above 1 in every field (VSWR 1 is a perfect match, which no band keeps); a limit of 1 saved by an earlier version opens as 2. Over a measured load the load is read per frequency from its model and the search stays inside the measured data: an open edge is clamped to the data and the readout says "Reaches the end of the data at 70 MHz and 130 MHz", and a design frequency outside the data has no usable band.

## 5. Response view

Plots versus frequency: gain and return loss (S21, S11), VSWR, input impedance, phase and group delay, with up to 8 markers, a cursor readout and a linear or log axis. The plots use the sweep range whether or not the sweep is on; without a usable range (a start or stop of zero, or the two equal) the view offers **Sweep 0.5 f to 2 f**. A start typed above the stop, or a stop below the start, is swapped when it is committed, with a message ("Swapped the sweep to 250 MHz to 1 GHz"); the Response view, Optimize band and Broadband read a range the same way. On a touch screen drag sideways on a plot to move the cursor, with its values in a row under the plot; a vertical swipe scrolls the page and a tap sets a marker. The S-Plot graphs work the same way. **Limit areas** are rectangles a curve must stay out of; breaches are flagged and the band optimizer can use them as its cost. **Overlays** store named snapshots of the curves (up to 10) for comparison.

Over a measured load (a datapoint sweep of a file) the plots span the measured band: the curves are the network on the load model, at least 401 points with every measured frequency among them, and the dots are the network on each measured row itself, so the residual of the model shows where a dot leaves its curve. A note over the plots names the model. **Load without network** in the toolbar adds |S11| and VSWR of the load without the network, dashed, to show what the network improves; the cursor and marker readout lists them too. With Log f the axis spans the data's own range, not whole decades. On a short window the view scrolls, so the readout table follows the four plots and never covers one.

On a phone a sideways drag on a plot moves the cursor, a tap sets a marker, and the readout row under each plot shows the values; the readout table with the cursor and marker columns follows the plots in the page, so it never covers a plot.

## 6. S-Plot view

An s-parameter viewer for Touchstone, CITI, EZNEC and VNA CSV files (add them with Add file, with Open in the File menu, or by dropping them on the view; several files at once give one message, such as "Added 2 files; broken.s2p could not be read: ...", and a `.txt` or `.csv` table above 20 MB is refused before it is read): file list with colours, graphs of s11, s12, s21, s22 in dB and phase, return loss, K and mu, MAG or MSG, a polar plot of s21 and s12, s11 and s22 on a Smith chart, and a gain table per frequency that highlights the selected row in every graph. Export converts to Z, Y, H or ABCD parameters (normalized or not) as Touchstone files or CITI to Touchstone. "To the Smith chart" sends s11 or s22 as the load or as a datapoint sweep, or uses the file as the two-port device for the circles.

**Add this design's network** (in the file list, or Add network to S-Plot in the File menu) plots the design's own network as a two-port referenced to Z0, as built (realized values, losses and packages when those are on), over the measured band when the design has a measured load, the sweep range when a frequency sweep is on, else 0.5 f to 2 f. The entry is marked live and follows your tuning a moment after each change; Adding it again replaces it, and **Freeze** keeps it as a snapshot so you can compare before and after. The S-Plot list is not part of the design, so Undo does not touch it; removing an entry asks nothing, and its message carries an Undo for 8 seconds that puts the entry back.

## 8. Analysis for building

- **Build** (a design tool next to Tolerance; on a phone in the Tools segment): how this network is made, saved with the design. It opens beside the chart, so the as-built locus moves while you type, and every change is one step of Undo with its name ("Undo: Changed the stackup to RO4350B, 0.762 mm", "Undo: Changed 0402: ESL to 450 pH", "Undo: Board on"). Its sections:
  - **Realization**: the value series (E6 to E96, My stock, Part library), the scope, the stock list when My stock is chosen, and **Part library…** with the number of parts in this browser.
  - **Losses**: the default Q of inductors and of capacitors (a Q on a card overrides them).
  - **Packages**: one card per package the network uses ("0402 chip · 3 parts") with ESL, Cp, ESR, the land size and the pad capacitance (computed from the lands and the stackup while the board is on, else the typed **Pads** value). A changed card says "changed for this design" and offers **Reset** to the built-in values. **Use 0402 for all parts** gives every L and C the 0402 package in one step; **Show all packages** lists the six. On a phone each package is a card that opens by a tap.
  - **Board**: **Include the board** (the same switch as in the network row), the laminate (the presets and your own laminates) with **Save as my laminate**, the stackup numbers, the vias and the traces, described below.
  - **Part ratings**: the largest peak voltage and RMS current per part kind; they need a source power P in the design strip.
  - At its foot: "Saved with this design.", **Use as default for new designs** (the board, the package models, the default Q and the series become what a new design starts with in this browser) and **Reset to defaults** (back to those defaults, as one step; part ratings and the stock list stay).
- **What travels with a design**: the build is part of the design, in the `.json` file, the share link and the autosave, so a colleague who opens your link sees the same as-built locus and exports the same `.s2p`, report and BOM layout notes, whatever their own defaults. A design saved before 0.6.0 has no build: it opens with this browser's defaults for new designs (the board and package table older versions kept in the browser, so it evaluates as it did there) and keeps them from then on; when that default board is on, the status line says so once ("Saved before the board travelled with designs: using this browser's default board (FR-4, 1.6 mm). Check it in Build."). A link made now still opens in 0.5.0, which ignores the build. A share link with a build is about 240 characters longer (about 360 with every board number changed). Examples, `.xmlsc` files, lessons and workflow starts take this browser's defaults for new designs when they come on screen and keep them, so a later **Use as default for new designs** or a change in Settings moves nothing on screen or in Undo; the old-design message has **Open Build**.
- **Package numbers** keep one range wherever they are typed or read: ESL up to 1 µH, Cp and pads up to 1 nF, ESR up to 1 kΩ, land length and width up to 10 cm. A default package table from 0.5.0 with a number outside it (0.5.0 had no upper limit, so "10" typed for 10 nH was kept as 10 H) has that number set back to the built-in one when it is read, and the start says which ("0805 ESL 10 H"), once.
- **An autosave that cannot be read** (made by a newer version, or damaged) is not written over: it is kept in this browser as a backup, the status line says why it could not be opened and **Save a copy** saves it as a design file, which File, Open reads (and says why, if this version still cannot open it); the start shows Example 5. A backup is never written over: where the browser has no room for the copy, or all five backups are in use, autosave pauses until you save it as a file (or clear a backup).
- **Backups in this browser**: Settings, Data in this browser, lists each backup. An autosave that could not be opened has **Save a copy** and **Clear…**; the design a share link replaced (see Share link) has **Open**, which opens it as a step that Undo takes back, **Save a copy** and **Clear…**. Up to five of each kind are kept.
- **Component packages**: choose a package per inductor or capacitor on its card (0201, 0402, 0603, 0805, 1206; wire-wound coil for inductors). The chart then draws a third, dotted locus with diamond markers for the network as built: a chip capacitor gets its series inductance, a chip inductor its parallel capacitance and series resistance, and every part the capacitance of its pads to ground. The legend under the chart toolbar names the three loci (ideal, realized, with packages); with Realize on, the packages are applied to the realized values. The card shows the part's self-resonant frequency, the Response view plots the as-built curves dotted, the report adds a datapoint table naming the packages, and the BOM gets a Package column. The package models with their typical values and a source note per package are in Build, Packages; edit them to match the parts you buy, for this design. Pick "none" on a card to leave a part ideal.
- **The board in the network**: turn **Board** on in the network row (or **Include the board** in Build) and the dotted as-built locus, the as-built Response curves, the `.s2p` export, the S-Plot network entry and the report include the board as well as the packages (the legend then says "With packages and the board", or "With the board" when no part names a package):
  - **Stackup**: a laminate preset (FR-4 1.6 mm and 0.8 mm after the Isola 370HR data sheet, Dk 4.17 and Df 0.0161 at 1 GHz; Rogers RO4003C 0.508 mm and RO4350B 0.762 mm with their design Dk 3.55 and 3.66 and Df 0.0027 and 0.0037 at 10 GHz; 1 oz copper with 3.2 µm RMS roughness, the figure Rogers gives for standard electrodeposited foil) or your own numbers: the dielectric height **h**, **εr** and **tanδ** at the frequency **at f**, the copper thickness **t** (0 is a perfect, infinitely thin strip without conductor loss) and its RMS roughness **Rq**. The Djordjevic-Sarkar model makes εr and tanδ causal over frequency: εr falls slowly (about 0.1 per decade for FR-4), tanδ stays within a few percent. The frequency **at f** must lie above 1 kHz and below 1 THz, the band of the model's relaxations (outside it the model goes far off: at 10 Hz FR-4 would come out with a negative εr at 1 GHz), and a loss tangent so high for its εr that the model's εr falls below 1 somewhere up to 110 GHz is refused (tanδ 0.3 on εr 4.17 at 1 GHz reaches 0.5 at 100 GHz). A refused entry says why next to the field and keeps the old value; a file or a share link with such a stackup is refused as a whole. Microstrip elements and the Dimensions dialog use the stackup whether or not the board is in the network. The preset's tooltip cites its data sheet.
  - **Pads**: each package's two lands to ground computed from the land size (the **Land l** and **Land w** fields of each package in Build: typical reflow lands, close to IPC-7351 nominal) and the stackup, in place of the fixed **Pads** value (Build shows the computed value in its place while the board is in the network); a package whose land length is 0 keeps the table value. A land is estimated as the parallel plate plus the fringing at its four edges (from the static microstrip model); for a land smaller than the board is thick (its equal-area side below h) that edge estimate runs low (19 % for an 0402 on 1.6 mm FR-4), so there the larger of it and the capacitance of a disc of the same area with the ground plane's image is taken. An 0402 on 1.6 mm FR-4 comes out at about 0.12 pF for both lands. The table's 0.03 pF is a typical figure, not computed from a stackup: it is lower than the land model gives over a solid ground plane, as for pads with the ground cleared under them. Small lands remain the least certain part of the board model; a field solver is the reference.
  - **Vias**: every shunt part (L, C, R or RLC block) reaches the ground plane through **Vias per shunt part** vias (default one) of the **Via drill** diameter, through the dielectric height, an inductance in series with the part (Goldfarb-Pucel: 0.54 nH for a 0.3 mm drill through 1.6 mm, 0.46 nH for 0.4 mm). Several vias are taken in parallel without their mutual inductance, so closely spaced vias are a little worse than shown.
  - **Traces between parts**: **Trace between parts** puts a microstrip of that length between every two neighbouring lumped parts (0, the default, leaves them out); its width is **Trace width**, or with 0 the width whose Z0 is the design's Z0 at the design frequency. A gap next to a line, stub, microstrip or transformer gets none: those are their own interconnect. The trace sits on the load side of the part it leads to, so the part's locus walks the trace first.
  - A line under the fields states the limits (circuit-level estimates: no EM, no coupling or discontinuities, the pad estimate may be low for small lands, vias without mutual inductance), with a help link to this section. The line under the fields gives the via inductance in nH, the 0402 pad capacitance (from the land size, or the table value without one), the trace length and width in use, and flags a trace width outside the dispersion model's W/h range. A stackup the model cannot use leaves the whole board out (no pads, vias or traces), and the line says why. The BOM lists the layout assumptions (stackup, pad model and values, vias per shunt part, trace length and width) under the parts, and the report adds them to the as-built datapoint table.
  - Not modelled: anything a field solver would see. No coupling between traces, pads or parts, no discontinuities (the step from a trace to a pad, bends, tees, the via's pad and antipad, the open end of a line), no radiation or surface waves, no pads on an R or RLC block (they have no package), no via at the end of a shorted stub, and the ground-side pad of a shunt part is taken as shorted by its via.
- **Stress**: with a source power set, each card shows peak voltage and RMS current; limits per part kind flag exceedances, and the BOM carries the same columns.
- **Tolerance (Monte Carlo)**: the packages' parasitics and the board (pads, vias, traces) are not in the run: it varies the ideal or realized part values only, and the panel says so. Random part values within their tolerances, N seeded runs in a worker; the cloud of input impedances on the chart, a VSWR histogram and the yield against a VSWR limit, judged towards the design target when one is set (towards Z0 otherwise), as Auto match, the spread ranking, the worst case and design centring judge. The default tolerance has one rule: the **Tolerance** field when typed, else, left empty, the tolerance of the series chosen in Build, Realization, whether Realize is on or off (the field's placeholder says it: "E24: 5 %", and the panel "Default 5 % from E24, set in Build"; a stock list counts as 5 %); 0 keeps every part exact. A part's own tolerance works the same way (empty is the default, 0 exact). Design centring and Auto match's yield take the same default. The limit and tolerance fields stay with no parts in the ladder, since Auto match judges its yield by them. Judged over the band with a measured load, each run is measured over the measured band against the load model (the cloud stays at the design frequency, on the nominal row); the yield says over what ("over the measured band, 70 MHz to 130 MHz"), the histogram follows right under it, and the next line gives the spread of the worst VSWR over the band (nominal, median, 95 % below, worst), with the design-frequency figures of the cloud and the histogram in a smaller line. When the design changes after a run, the cloud fades with "stale" written over it and a note with **Run again** sits above the old result, which is dimmed (the only Run again then); the result keeps naming the range it was judged over. On the phone at half, the result scrolls into view when a run ends, from the yield line down to the histogram. Tolerance sits among the design tools, next to Auto match and Optimize band. **Try**: [Check the match is robust](workflow:robust).
  - **Yield interval**: the yield comes with its Wilson 95 % interval ("Yield 44.6 % (446 of 1000 runs ...; 95 % interval 41.5 to 47.7 %)"): with 1000 runs a yield is known to about 3 %, so a change smaller than that is not a change. More runs narrow it.
  - **Reel correlation**: one coefficient per part kind (L, C, R; the parts of an RLC block count by their kind), 0 (independent, the default) to 1 (all parts of the kind drift as one). Parts from one reel drift together; the draws of any two parts of a kind then have that correlation, uniform or normal as chosen (a normal factor shared by the kind per run, turned into a uniform draw through the normal distribution so the correlation is exact). A value outside 0 to 1 is refused with a message naming the field ("Reel correlation C: "1.5" is above the maximum 1"). Correlation widens the spread when parts of a kind act the same way and narrows it when they offset each other. With every coefficient 0 a seed gives the same cloud as before.
  - **Which part matters most**: live, without a run, the panel ranks the varied parts by their share of the spread: "Shunt L 1 contributes 58 % of the spread" (or "The three parts share the spread about equally" when every share is within a fifth of an equal one), then a table with each part's tolerance, its **VSWR per %** and its share as a bar. The share is the part's slope times its tolerance, as a part of the variance of the quantity the yield is judged by, linearized from analytic derivatives through the network: at the design frequency the VSWR, and **VSWR per %** is its change for a 1 % change of the part there; over the band the smooth band VSWR, a power mean of order 32 of the VSWR across the band (smooth where the plain worst case has kinks, weighted to the frequencies where the VSWR is worst), and **VSWR per %** is the change of that mean. The phase of Γ does not move the VSWR away from a match, so it is not counted: a part that turns Γ round without changing its size ranks low. At a perfect match the VSWR has no slope (the column reads 0), and very near one the VSWR is not linear in the parts; the shares then use the mean square change of Γ, the second-order term of the mismatch at a match, and the note under the table says so. The shares add up to 100 %; with reel correlation a part whose drift offsets another's gets a negative share. A part typed as 0 has nothing to vary and is left out, and a band point with total reflection (an active measured row) is left out of the power mean. The network ranked is the one analysed: realized values with Realize on, the loss model, the target, the measured load model over the band when there is one. While the Tolerance panel is open each ladder card shows its part's share as a small bar ("58 % of the spread").
  - **Worst case**: each run also reports its worst case, the largest judged VSWR of the nominal network, the worst corner (every part at one end of its tolerance, in the direction that hurts) and the run's own Monte Carlo samples, and says which it is: "Worst case: VSWR 1.615 at a corner, with Shunt L 1 -5 %, Series C 2 -5 %". The corner directions come from the sensitivities and every candidate is evaluated in the full network; with up to 10 varied quantities every corner is evaluated as well, and the line says whether the sensitivities found the worst one. A corner is a bound only when the response is monotonic in every part across the tolerance band. Where it is not (a resonance or a peak inside the band: a line whose length tolerance swings the impedance through its peak, a trap tuned at f, a resistor that is best at its nominal value, low-Q parts with a wide tolerance) the nominal network or a draw inside the band is worse than every corner, and the line names it ("Worst case: VSWR 4.000 at the nominal values; the worst corner reaches 3.985 ...", or "in run 1185, inside the tolerance band (...)") and says that a corner is not a bound there. A normal draw (the band is 3 sigma) can also go past a corner. Correlation does not change the corners. The part names come with the run, so they still fit after the design changes.
  - **Design centring**: **Centre for yield** moves the nominal values to where the most runs pass, within each part's realizable range (the band optimizer's bounds, and half to twice the value), with the tolerances, limit and band of the panel. It compares candidates on one fixed set of 300 seeded draws (so the comparison is fair and repeatable), moves towards the centre of the passing draws, and then walks a smoothed yield with Nelder-Mead in log coordinates, in a worker with a progress bar and **Stop**. With Realize on, the centred values snap afterwards: each part takes the series or stock value just below or just above its centred value, or keeps its value, whichever combination passes most. The progress bar counts every yield evaluation and says what the search is doing (searching for the centre, snapping to E24, checking on fresh runs). The yield before and after is then measured again on 2000 fresh draws, with intervals, and the result names the range it judged: "Yield 45.8 % (43.6 to 47.9 %) → 93.2 % (92.0 to 94.2 %) on 2000 fresh runs within VSWR 1.38 over the sweep range, 900 MHz to 1.1 GHz". Under it come the nominal worst VSWR before and after (what the centre trades: it may give up some of the nominal match for the yield), the yield of the centred values before snapping, the parts whose shown value changes ("Shunt L 1 11 nH → 12 nH"), and any part that sits at the edge of its range (half or twice its value, or its realizable bound), where the centre may lie further out. **Apply** is offered only when the gain is more than 2000 runs can resolve, the after interval above the before interval (or the gain above the two half-widths together); it sets the listed parts, and only those, in one undo step named after the gain ("Centred the design: yield 45.8 % to 93.2 % within VSWR 1.38"). With Realize on, a part whose realized value stays keeps its own value. Otherwise the panel says "No better centre found", with the yield of the best centre when it is higher but within what the runs can tell apart. Any change of the design, its limit or its band after a search puts a note with **Centre again** above the old result, which is dimmed, whether it found a better centre or not; the old result keeps naming the limit and range it was found for. Locked parts and RLC blocks stay as they are.

**Try**: the tour [Losses and component packages](tour:packages) turns the package locus on; then tick **Board in the network as built** in Settings to see what the pads, vias and traces add. [Example 15](example:Example15.xmlsc) has a lossy line.

- **Where to get the files**: the dialog links each manufacturer's download page (Murata capacitors and inductors, Coilcraft, Johanson Technology, KEMET's K-SIM, TDK capacitors and inductors). Their terms allow simulation and not redistribution, so the app ships none of their files. **Add synthetic examples** adds ten made-up 0402 parts (named SYNTH-, manufacturer "Synthetic (not a real part)") generated from equivalent circuits, to try the library without downloading anything; they are not real parts.
- **How it was measured**: manufacturers measure a chip part in series between the two ports (series-through) or from the line to ground (shunt-through). The app tells them apart from the data (series-through keeps S11 + S21 = 1, shunt-through S21 - S11 = 1) and says so when it cannot; change **Measured** in the list ("in series" or "to ground") if the detection is wrong. Data that cannot be read the chosen way (series-through data has no shunt admittance at all) is refused with the reason.
- **In the network**: **Add series** or **Add shunt** in the list adds the part at the source end. Its card shows the part number and manufacturer, the effective value and Q at the design frequency ("C eff 11.8 pF, Q 67.4 at 1 GHz"; above its resonance a capacitor acts as an inductor and the card says so), the self-resonance, and a placement switch (Undo names the change: "Placed part 2 (SYNTH-C0402-10P) to ground, was in series"). Data with a negative resistance at the design frequency (measurement noise on a high-Q part) is flagged "not passive" instead of showing a Q. The point after a library part on the chart follows its data and cannot be dragged; the status line says so. A series part from a series-through file uses the measured two-port itself; every other placement uses the part impedance from the data (Z = B of the chain matrix for a series-through file, 1 / C for a shunt-through file). A shunt part from a series-through file lacks the ground path and the grounded pad a shunt mounting adds, and a series part from a shunt-through file keeps the fixture's ground path in its impedance: the card flags both. With the board in the network a shunt library part gets its via and the traces between parts like any other part; library parts have no package.
- **Between and beyond the data**: between the file's frequencies the S-parameters are interpolated linearly in real and imaginary parts (bounded, keeps passive data passive, and what scikit-rf does; on the synthetic files within 2e-4 of the generating circuit). Outside them the part has no value: nothing is extrapolated, the chart and plots leave those frequencies out, the card says "No value at 20 GHz: above the part's data (10 MHz to 10 GHz)", the readouts and tables say "no value at 20 GHz", the status line names the part and its data range, sweep summaries leave those points out, the Response view notes the region without data, and the `.s2p` export refuses a range that reaches past the data (with the sweep off it names the export range, 0.5 f to 2 f, and how to set another).
- **ESR and Q between the samples**: they come from the linearly interpolated S-parameters, not from the part itself, so between two samples they can differ slightly from the true values (an ideal lossless inductor measured an octave apart reads a Q of about 22 half way between the samples; denser data narrows the difference). At the samples they are the measured ones.
- **Files and links**: a `.json` design file carries the measured data of the parts it uses, so it reopens complete anywhere. A share link carries each part's names and a fitted equivalent circuit only (a capacitor as C with ESL and ESR, an inductor as L with series R across Cp): when the recipient's library has the part, its data is used; otherwise the card says "Approximate: the fitted equivalent circuit" until the file is loaded. The library's part is only taken when it is the same kind (C or L) and its fitted value is within 10 % of the one the link carries; otherwise the fitted circuit stays, and the card and the status line say why. Copying a link with library parts (or with Realize from the part library) says that they travel as fitted circuits and suggests the `.json` file; opening such a link says how many parts arrived approximate, how many took the data from your library, and how many L and C Realize finds no part for in your library.
- **What uses the data and what does not**: the chart, the Response view, the S-Plot network entry, the `.s2p` export, the stress columns and the BOM use the measured data. The SPICE and LTspice exports write the fitted equivalent circuit and say so in their comments and in the status line; the Touchstone CKT netlist writes it as an RLC statement with a comment (a lossless inductor fit gets a parallel R of 1e12 ohm, an open, since R=0 would be a short); `.xmlsc` cannot hold a library part and refuses with a message naming the element, and with only parts Realize chose it saves the ideal values and says so. The BOM gives a library part its catalogue value under Realized (the nominal value read from the data, rounded to two significant digits) and its effective value at the design frequency under Effective at f0; an L or C for which the library has no part reads "no library part" and keeps its ideal value, as its card says ("no library part, stays ideal"). Text in the BOM that a spreadsheet would read as a formula (starting with =, +, -, @) is written with a leading apostrophe. The report lists the library parts with manufacturer and part number, marked measured or approximate. Monte Carlo, the spread ranking and design centring do not vary a library part (it is its measured data), and the matching tools and the optimizer do not tune it.
- **Realize from the part library**: with **Part library** as the source, each unlocked L and C in scope is replaced by the library part of its kind whose effective reactance at the design frequency is nearest (not the nominal value: a 10 pF part close to its resonance acts as a much larger one), and of the two parts that bracket the ideal reactance the one with the lower VSWR is kept, as for E series values. Only parts whose data covers the design frequency and that are below their resonance there are candidates. Resistors and RLC blocks stay exact, the pairs scope snaps each part on its own, and design centring leaves the centred values ideal. The realized line of a card names the part, its effective value at the design frequency and its deviation from the ideal value; the BOM's Manufacturer and Part number columns are filled for library parts.

### Measure and retune

**Try**: [Measure, compare, retune](workflow:measure-retune). When the board is built, measure its S11 on a VNA and set it against the design: what moved, by how much, and which part to change.

- **Compare with the design**: open the measured file (File, Open: Touchstone `.s1p` or `.s2p`, CITI or VNA CSV) and press **Compare with the design** in the import dialog. The dialog says which is which: Compare is the board you built, measured; **Use all rows as the load** is the load, measured. With Compare the load stays as it is: the file becomes the design's measured board, the input of the network as built. A one-port file is taken as measured with the load connected at port 2. A two-port file is taken as the network measured with port 2 on the VNA's reference (its S11 with port 2 terminated in the file's Z0), and the fit evaluates the model the same way; the device panel the import filled from it is put back, and one Undo goes back to the design before the file was opened. A file of three or more ports is refused with the reason (save S11 or the two-port from the VNA). The points are taken by the rule every reader of a saved design uses: finite positive frequencies, impedances up to an open (|S11| = 1 at 0°), one point per frequency (a segmented sweep repeats the joint: the last row is kept, and the message says how many), sorted; what Compare takes is what a saved file gives back. It needs a network and two usable rows: otherwise the button is off and says why. Measured points outside the frequencies the design's load is known at (a measured load's band) are left out and counted. **Stop comparing** in Build removes the measurement.
- **What is checked first**: the design frequency against the measured span (outside: "is this the measurement of this design?"), the target band against it (outside: measure over the band; partly outside: the frequencies not measured are not judged), and the design's own worst VSWR over the band on its load with no drift. A design that misses its target as built is flagged in warning style in Build and the status line: a retune would then correct the design as well as the board.
- **The overlay**: the Response view shows a panel **Measured board** at the top: |S11| in dB as measured (solid, drawn on top), as designed (the network as built, dashed grey), as fitted (thin cyan) and expected after a retune (dash-dot olive) when there are, and |ΔΓ| between measured and designed on the right axis, at the measured frequencies. The line over it gives |ΔΓ| at its worst and as an RMS, and the worst VSWR over the band, measured against designed and the target (the VSWR limit of the design; the band is Tolerance's band, else the sweep, else f). For a two-port measurement the measured VSWR is not on the design's load and is not given. Moving over the plot reads every curve at one frequency. The chart draws the same curves in the same colours (one palette, none of them an element colour or the dotted as-built style), the measured points as dots with start, stop and f marked; hovering a dot (or tapping it on a touch screen) shows a readout on the chart: its frequency, |S11| in dB, VSWR and Zin in Ω. The legend draws each curve's own line (designed in long dashes, after retune dash-dot), so none looks like the Realized curve (short dashes). The legend names each curve, on the phone too.
- **Fit what moved** (Build, Measured board): a least squares fit of the network as built to the measured S11, in a worker with its progress and **Stop**. **Parameters to fit** lists what can move, each with its nominal and its typical drift: every part value (±10 %), the pads of each inductor and capacitor (±0.2 pF added to both lands; half acts at each end of a series part, half at the node of a shunt part), all pads together (±50 %), the vias (±50 %), the traces between parts (±1 mm), the board's εr and height (±10 %, offered only where something depends on them: the pads, vias and traces of the board, or a microstrip, whose stackup follows the board's) and the **Reference plane** (typical ±20 ps one way, taken anywhere within ±1 ns, about 20 cm of cable: the VNA's calibration plane ahead of the board's input, a port extension; it belongs to the bench, not the board, so it is never called implausible, and the retune and the target figures leave it out; the fit starts it from a scan of the plane alone and from the phase slope of the measurement against the design). A library part has a value of its own (a scale on its measured impedance, typical ±5 %, approximate) and, on the board, its pads (±0.2 pF from none: the data is of the part, the lands are the board's). With the board off no pad parameter is listed. The part values, the pads of parts that have pads, the vias and the traces are ticked by default; all pads together, εr, h and the reference plane are offered unticked. Each parameter is scaled by its typical drift, and a small regularization towards no drift, scaled to each parameter's own effect, keeps the problem well conditioned without shrinking a weak parameter; the derivative of a part value is the analytic one of the sensitivity ranking, the board parameters take central differences. The fit compares Γ at the design's Z0, every measured frequency weighted alike.
- **What the data cannot tell apart**: before fitting, the parameters are ranked by how much of their effect is not already explained by the ones before them; a parameter whose effect is like that of one ranked before it (a correlation above 0.9987: less than 5 % of it differs), or that has no effect, is fixed at no drift and named: "Vias: not resolved: acts like Shunt L 1 value (correlation 0.9998 with it)" (a via is an inductance in series with the shunt L, so over a narrow band the two are one). After the fit a parameter whose uncertainty is half its typical drift or more is also fixed and named ("not resolved: its uncertainty is half its typical drift or more"), and the rest fitted again. More unknowns than the data supports are refused: N frequencies give 2N numbers, and a fit takes at most 2N - 2 unknowns ("5 unknowns from 3 frequencies is more than the data supports (at most 4 ...)"). Fewer than three usable frequencies are refused too.
- **The drift table**: every parameter: what moved, its nominal, the fitted drift with as many digits as its uncertainty needs ("+8.018 %", "+0.2981 pF") and its standard uncertainty (one sigma, from the Jacobian and the residual: it assumes independent errors of equal size and a model that can explain the data). A drift within two of its uncertainties of none is marked not significant; a fitted pair correlated above 0.95 says so ("correlation 0.997 with ..."), and a note under the table says what it means: when one of the two is not significant, "X or Y: these two cannot be told apart by this data" (the data fixes their joint effect, not which moved); when both are resolved, that they move together and each ± includes it. An unticked parameter reads "not fitted (unticked)". Under the table: |ΔΓ| RMS before and after the fit, the number of frequencies, the floor the residual is held against and the condition number. A change of the network, the build, the load, the part library or the measurement after a fit says "fit again"; so does a change of the ticks.
- **The checks of a fit**, under the table, in the Response panel and in the status line, as a senior engineer would make them before acting on it:
  - the floor: the noise of the measurement, from its point-to-point scatter (each point against the straight line through its neighbours, the median taken), or the accuracy of the VNA, whichever is larger. A VNA's calibration leaves a smooth error the scatter does not show: **VNA class** under the file's name sets it, lab (0.002, the default) or hobby, e.g. NanoVNA (0.02, taken only when the file names a NanoVNA, LibreVNA or tinySA in its name, its comments or its header; a plain table of frequency, re and im is held to the lab floor; the Compare message says which class and where to change it; one step of Undo, kept with the measurement, and a fit on show is fitted again against the new floor). The ± of every drift is taken against this floor too (and, for an averaged trace whose residual is correlated from point to point, widened for it). The text names the floor it used ("0.7 times the lab VNA accuracy floor 0.002 (the scatter between points, 0.0012, is below it)"). Up to 3 times the floor the model explains the board; up to 10 times it explains most of it but leaves a pattern the drifts take part of, so the fit is not acted on; above 10 times "the model does not explain this board": check the reference plane (when it is not ticked), the calibration, the file, the design, or an assembly fault (a wrong or misplaced part);
  - the reference plane is searched over its whole range (±1 ns, less when the frequency steps are coarse), with the other drifts fitted again at every plane, and the best of the minima kept; when another plane with other drifts fits the data about as well (inside the 99 % region), the fit is not acted on: "the measured band is too narrow to tell a reference-plane offset from part drift; measure over a wider band or calibrate at the board". The text under the table states the range;
  - with **Reference plane** not ticked, every fit is run again with it and the two compared (an F-test against the floor): when the plane explains the data better by more than chance (p below 0.001) and is at least twice its own uncertainty, or when it would move a drift by more than twice that drift's uncertainty, the check says so with its size, "a reference-plane offset of about +30.8 ps (about 5.1 mm of FR-4 microstrip) explains this measurement better: tick Reference plane and fit again", and the fit is not acted on until then. A small plane offset (1 to 2 mm of microstrip) otherwise passes as noise with wrong drifts. The advice is never given when the plane is ticked or when the design frequency lies outside the data;
  - the board was built from the source the design uses now: Compare keeps what the board was built from ("E24 values", "the part library"), and after a change the check says "this measurement was taken on a board built from E24 values; the design now uses the part library";
  - a fitted drift larger than 3 typical drifts is not one of a board built as designed, and one past what is physical (a pad capacitance or trace length below zero, a value scaled to almost nothing) is named as such; the reference plane is judged only against its range (±1 ns);
  - the design frequency must lie inside the measured span;
  - the ± of each drift includes what the reference plane allows when it is not ticked (it is the bench's, not known to be none); with a microstrip in the network, εr and h are checked the same way and named when they explain the data as well as a plane;
  - two parameters the data cannot tell apart are named as "X or Y" (one set aside, or one uncertain by more than a third of its typical drift): their drift is a joint effect, not a finding about either; pairs that merely move together but are each resolved share one line ("X did not move and Y moved by ...").
  A fit that fails one of these checks is not acted on: its drifts are shown in warning style, each reason is said once in Build, the Response panel and the status line, and the retune is refused with the first reason ("the design frequency (450 MHz) lies outside the measurement (700 MHz to 1.3 GHz)").
- **Suggest retune**: the part values, snapped to the design's series (E6 to E96), your stock or the part library (Realize's source; with Realize off, the series it would snap to), that bring the built board as fitted back inside the VSWR limit over the band, on the design's load (also for a two-port measurement), judged only at the band frequencies inside the measured span, and refuses when part of the target band was not measured: a change judged on part of the band can make the rest worse, so measure the board over the whole band first. A board inside the target already needs no change, and the suggestion says so. A fitted value drift stays with its place (a part changed to another value keeps the drift of the one it replaces: the pads, the via and the reel around it are the board's); a library part is taken as measured. Every single change is tried; when none reaches the target, every pair (with more than three parts, the pairs among the seven values nearest each part first, then over all their values); when none does, all parts by turns. That result is pruned to the changes it needs, and when more than two are left the pairs among those parts are tried again over all their values: the suggestion is the fewest changes that reach the target. It shows each change ("Shunt L 1: 10 nH to 9.1 nH"), the expected worst VSWR against the old one and the target, which part to change first ("One change is enough: change Shunt L 1", or with several, the one that helps most alone), and whether the design as built met the target and the board drifted out of it, or the design missed it already. Locked parts are not changed. It is a search, not a proof that no better combination exists; without a fit it retunes the board as designed. Sixteen parts over 201 frequencies take well under two seconds. A change that does not make the board better than it is is never suggested: then "no change of the parts in reach does better than the board as it is" with what to try next (another series, more stock or library parts, Optimize band), and there is no Apply. Every suggestion is checked against the other explanations the data allows (the drifts at the edges of their uncertainty, the pairs it cannot tell apart swapped, the fit with the plane or the laminate added): it is offered only when it leaves the board no worse than it is under each, and it says so ("inside the target under every explanation the data allows", or "up to 1.37 under the other explanations; never worse than the board as it is"); when no change is safe it says "no change is safe" and why. The cause names each drift the retune compensates and whether it is within its part's tolerance. A retune inside the target with little to spare (1.362 against 1.38) says so: it compensates this board's drift, and a new batch needs the cause of a drift beyond its tolerance fixed rather than the retune copied (a drift within tolerance may come out otherwise on the next batch: measure it).
- **Apply retune**: changes the parts as one step of Undo ("Retuned Shunt L 1 to 9.1 nH"), placing exactly what the retune evaluated: a series or stock value as the part's value, a library part as the library part (with Realize from the library, a part Realize maps the value back to keeps the design ideal); Realize compares neighbouring values on the whole network, so a change can make it pick another value for a part the retune did not change: Apply keeps such a part as the board has it (locked where Realize would still move it) and the status line names it; Apply is refused, with the reason, when the network as built would still differ from the suggestion. The status line after Apply gives the worst VSWR the reworked board should measure. In the workflow, a step about a fit, a retune or an Apply that is no longer there (after Undo, New, an example or another file) moves back to the step that fits, with a note. The measurement is still of the board before the retune: the curves then set it against the retuned design, and Build says so and what the reworked board should measure ("a worst VSWR of about 1.362 at 1.1 GHz"). Rework the board, measure it and compare again.
- **Saved with the design**: the measurement is part of the design, in the `.json` file (File, Save as Smith Online and every other `.json` writer: at full precision, up to 2001 points) and the autosave. A share link carries it up to 201 points (rounded like the rest of the link); a larger one is left out and the Share message says so, and a link the measurement makes long (over 4000 characters) says that some mail and chat tools cut long links and that a `.json` file is safer to send. A measurement that cannot be read (no points, values that are not numbers) is left out when the design opens; the design itself always opens. The fit and the retune are results of this session and are not saved.
- **The sample**: Learn, Sample files, **Built board at 1 GHz (synthetic .s1p)** is the board of the workflow's start design (the buildable board with the shunt L re-centred to 10 nH for the 0402 parts and the board, series C 2.7 pF, on FR-4 1.6 mm, one via, on 100 - j50 Ω; as built it meets VSWR 1.38 over 0.9 to 1.1 GHz, worst 1.298) with known drifts, +8 % on the shunt L and +0.3 pF on the pads of the series C, which take it out of the target (1.550), and noise of 0.001 in Γ, 121 points from 0.7 to 1.3 GHz. It is made from the model by `scripts/synth-board.mjs`, not measured. The fit gives back +8.02 % ± 0.04 % and +0.298 pF ± 0.001 pF with the residual at the noise floor, and names the via; the retune to 9.1 nH brings the worst VSWR from 1.550 to 1.362, inside 1.38. The oracle recomputes all of it with scikit-rf and scipy (`tools/oracle/d6_check.py`).
- **Limits**: the fit can only find what the model has: a drift the model has no parameter for (a part placed wrong, a cracked joint, coupling, a ground return) shows as a residual well above the measurement noise, and the checks then say the model does not explain the board. The reference plane is a matched, lossless delay: a fixture with loss or a mismatched launch is not modelled, calibrate to the board's input where you can; ticking it can make pads that act like a phase shift unresolvable. Library parts have no value parameter (they are their data); their pads, vias and traces can still be fitted.

## 9. Files, export and sharing

- **Open**: `.xmlsc` (Smith V4.1 projects, all 22 original examples are also in Learn, Smith V4.1 examples), Smith Online `.json`, Touchstone `.s1p` to `.s4p`, CITI `.citi` or `.cti`, EZNEC `Lastz.txt`, VNA CSV exports (`.csv`, `.txt`, see below).
- **VNA CSV and tables** (`.csv` and `.txt`, in Open and in the S-Plot view's Add file):
  - Keysight/Agilent ENA and PNA exports: `!` header lines, then `BEGIN CH1_DATA` ... `END` blocks with a column header such as `Freq(Hz),S11(REAL),S11(IMAG)`; several traces per block and several blocks of the same sweep are merged (a row dropped in one block is dropped in all), and a block the reader cannot use, or one at other frequencies, is skipped with a note.
  - Any comma, semicolon, tab or space separated table with a header row: a frequency column (`Freq`, `Frequency`, `f`, with the unit in the header such as `Freq [MHz]` or on the numbers such as `1.5 GHz`; Hz when neither says) and S11 to S22 as re/im, mag/angle or dB/angle pairs (`S11 dB`, `S11 Phase (deg)`, `S21_real`, `S(2,1)`, `dB(S(2,1))` as HFSS and ADS write it; angles in degrees unless the header says rad), or `R` and `X` (also `Impedance Real`, `Zin Re`) columns for an impedance in ohm. A header written as a `#` comment above the rows, as numpy writes it, is used. Y, Z, H and G parameter columns, admittance, transmission, return loss, SWR and delay columns are skipped with a note; the import dialog lists the notes, the S-Plot file tooltip carries them, and a refusal names them.
  - NanoVNA-Saver and LibreVNA style tables: frequency, re, im triples per trace, value columns named only Re and Im, Mag and Phase or Gamma Real and Gamma Imag (a column such as `Phase` joins the parameter before it, else it is taken as S11, then S21; the same parameter given as re/im and as magnitude and angle is read once, from re/im, with a note), and headerless `f re im` rows.
  - Lines starting with `!` or `#` are comments. With semicolons, tabs or spaces a comma inside a number is a decimal comma; in a comma separated file a quoted cell such as `"0,5"` may hold one, `"1,000,000"` is read with thousands separators, and an ambiguous `"1,400"` is not read (the row is dropped). The reference impedance is read from a line such as `Reference impedance: 75 Ohm`, `Z0 = 75` or `# Hz S RI R 75`, else 50 Ω is assumed and the description says so. Rows are sorted by frequency. Rows at zero, negative or non-finite frequency, rows repeating a frequency and rows with a value that is not a number (or one too large to convert) are dropped, and the description says how many and why. A file without a frequency column, without S-parameter or R and X columns, or with fewer than two usable rows is refused with a message that names what is missing.
  - Touchstone text saved as `.csv` or `.txt` (an option line such as `# MHz S MA R 50` or a bare `#` above plain rows, or a Touchstone 2 `[Version]` keyword) is read as Touchstone, with its unit and format; a faulty option line such as `# GHz S MA R 50 Ohm` is reported by the Touchstone reader. A `#` line that is a column header makes the file a table.
  - A file with S11 only goes to the import dialog: the row nearest the design frequency is highlighted, a click makes a row the load, Use all rows as the load makes a datapoint sweep. A file with S21, S12 or S22 as well also goes to the S-Plot list (Show in S-Plot in the dialog); the parameters it lacks show as gaps, the file card and the gain table name them, and Use as device and Save Touchstone need all four. A file with S11, S21, S12 and S22 fills the two-port panel as a Touchstone file does. A file without S11 goes to the S-Plot list only, and the status line says so.
  - A `.txt` file is tried as a VNA table first and as an EZNEC `Lastz.txt` second (an EZNEC table, recognized by its `Src #` column, goes to the EZNEC reader); when neither reads it the message gives both reasons.
- **Save**: Smith Online `.json` (everything, the build and the measured data of library parts included), Smith V4.1 `.xmlsc` (L, C, lines and stubs, user datapoints; opens in the original).
- **Export**: chart as SVG or PNG, datapoints as CSV, Touchstone CKT netlist, ASCII datapoints and circles in the original's layout, bill of materials (Realize on; a Package column when parts name one, a Width column for microstrips, and the layout assumptions under the table when a part names a package or the board is in the network), SPICE `.cir`, LTspice `.asc`, the network as `.s2p` (as built, with realized values, losses, packages and, when it is in the network, the board, over the measured band of a measured load or the sweep when a frequency sweep is on, else 0.5 f to 2 f; its header says what it contains: ideal or realized values, the loss model, package parasitics, the board and its stackup; SPICE and LTspice use the same range but leave the package parasitics and the board out, and say so in a comment at the top when a part names a package or the board is in the network: "Component package parasitics are not included; see the .s2p export", or the same for the board's pads, vias and traces), Add network to S-Plot (the same network as a live S-Plot entry, see section 6; not undoable, as the S-Plot list is not part of the design), and a Report (chart, schematic, datapoints, circles as one SVG or a print page). Print prints the last report selection; when the browser blocks pop-ups it falls back to the native print dialog of the page and says so. Copy chart as image and copy datapoints as text go to the clipboard. The Export section is grouped: For a simulator (`.s2p`, SPICE, LTspice, netlist), For the build (bill of materials, report, print; the bill of materials is listed with Realize off too, greyed with the reason), Compare in the app (Add network to S-Plot), and Images and data. **Try**: [Verify against an independent tool](workflow:verify).
- **Share link** puts the whole design in the URL, its build (board and package models) included, and copies the link; on the desktop the button shows a check and **Copied** for a moment once it is on the clipboard, keeping its width; on the phone (Share link in the More sheet) the toast confirms it. The link is not left in the address bar, so a reload opens your autosaved work and not the design as it was shared; where the browser refuses the clipboard, the link is put in the address bar to copy from, and a reload of that tab opens the autosave when it holds that design or newer work, and the link when the autosave could not save it (autosave paused, storage full). Opening a link, at the start or by pasting it into the address bar of the running app, puts the design on screen as a step; the status line names the design, and when your own design was there, Undo brings it back and it is kept in this browser as a backup (Settings, Data in this browser, opens it again after the tab is closed). The link leaves the address bar once the autosave holds its design, so a reload before that opens it again. A link that is not a design says so and opens nothing. A `.json` file that cannot be opened says why: an empty file, not JSON, not a design file, or the part that is not usable ("the board in it is not usable: the height h is outside 1 µm to 100 mm").

## 11. Known limitations

- Library parts: the fixture is detected from two identities of an ideal fixture; real files with large fixture parasitics may read as neither, and the app then asks you to check it. The fitted equivalent circuit of a share link is approximate (a real part's ESR rises with frequency and its value drifts); the measured data is used whenever the part is in your library or the design came as a `.json` file. Passivity of measured data is not enforced, and measured S12 and S21 are averaged. Only the synthetic examples have been read in the tests: no manufacturer's file was loaded while this was written.

- The package parasitics are typical values per package size, not the data of a particular part; Build, Packages is the place to enter datasheet figures. The SPICE, LTspice, netlist and `.xmlsc` exports carry the values and losses but not the parasitics or the board, and Monte Carlo, stress and the matching tools work on the network without them.
- The board model is circuit-level, not electromagnetic: microstrip lines and traces are isolated lines (no coupling to each other or to the parts), with no discontinuity models, and the pad and via figures are closed-form estimates (pads possibly low for lands much smaller than the board is thick; vias without mutual inductance). The microstrip model is a port of scikit-rf's `MLine`: its static formulas are stated within 0.2 % (Hammerstad-Jensen), the εeff dispersion within 0.6 % for 0.1 ≤ W/h ≤ 100, εr ≤ 20, h/λ0 ≤ 0.13 (Kirschning-Jansen), and for the Z0 dispersion (Jansen-Kirschning) the Qucs technical documentation gives the range 0.1 ≤ W/h ≤ 10, εr ≤ 18, h/λ0 ≤ 0.1 and no accuracy figure; the card flags a line outside that last range. Against Keysight ADS on a 25 mm FR-4 line from 0.2 to 20 GHz the model is within 0.014 dB and 0.26° in S21. The conductor loss formula is optimistic where the copper is thinner than about three skin depths (below about 30 MHz for 35 µm copper).
- The LTspice `.asc` writer has not been opened in LTspice; the SPICE netlist and the `.s2p` writer are covered by parse-back tests.
- The fitted model of a measured load is made passive from 10 % below to 10 % above the data, the range the band tools can reach (they hold the model 5 % past the data). Inside that range the check is exact rather than a grid: the frequencies where |Γ| crosses 1 come from the eigenvalues of the model's Hamiltonian matrix, and the check points include them and the points between them, besides a grid of 20 points per row (at least 2001) with each local maximum refined. Outside that range, and at infinity (|d|, which is reported but not enforced), the model is not made passive, since the app never evaluates it there. Violations are removed by adjusting the residues and d with the least change at the rows; the passive model that misses the rows least is kept, and when that adjustment would miss them by more than scaling the original fit down to |Γ| ≤ 1, the scaled fit is used instead (which shows as a larger residual, and then as linear interpolation when it passes 0.01).
- The Bode-Fano limit of a fitted load is exact only for a load whose model is one reactance with a resistance across the band; otherwise it is an estimate from the load Q at the band centre (the model's exact slope, not a finite difference of noisy rows), which can be off either way for a load of low Q or with several resonances in the band. The real frequency technique of depth phase D7 is the answer for those.
- The usable bandwidth search keeps its documented fallback (a search range that excludes the centre frequency is widened, not refused) and may miss a spike narrower than its coarse step.
- Changing the design frequency without a frequency sweep on is accepted without a word; the Response view keeps its own range.
- When a project whose start datapoint is not among the sweep loads is saved as `.xmlsc`, the start is added to the loads of the file.
- A typed value or a switch does not describe itself in the status line (it clears it); Undo and Redo name it.
- Pressing Escape in the Monte Carlo panel clears the cloud; run it again to redraw.
